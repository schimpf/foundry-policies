MB to GB Conversion Chart for Phone Storage

Phone storage is sold in gigabytes, but files are often listed in megabytes. This page gives you a chart, the formula, and a calculator to convert between them. It also explains why the numbers on your phone never seem to add up.

MB to GB chart

In decimal units, 1 GB is 1,000 MB. Divide megabytes by 1,000 to get gigabytes.

Storage units and bytes

Each step up is a factor of 1,000.

UnitShortBytes
KilobyteKB1,000
MegabyteMB1,000,000
GigabyteGB1,000,000,000
TerabyteTB1,000,000,000,000

These are the SI prefixes. The kilo, mega, giga and tera prefixes mean powers of ten (International Bureau of Weights and Measures, *The International System of Units (SI)*, 9th edition, 2019).

The formulas

  • MB to GB: GB = MB ÷ 1,000
  • GB to MB: MB = GB × 1,000
  • GB to TB: TB = GB ÷ 1,000
  • KB to MB: MB = KB ÷ 1,000
  • Bytes to GB: GB = bytes ÷ 1,000,000,000

Worked examples

  1. A 2,500 MB video. 2,500 ÷ 1,000 = 2.5 GB.
  2. A 4.7 GB download. 4.7 × 1,000 = 4,700 MB.
  3. Three 850 MB files. 3 × 850 = 2,550 MB. 2,550 ÷ 1,000 = 2.55 GB.
  4. Will it fit? You have 1.8 GB left and want to install a 1,400 MB update. 1,400 ÷ 1,000 = 1.4 GB. 1.8 − 1.4 = 0.4 GB left, so it fits.

GB or GiB: why your phone seems to lose space

Some software counts in powers of 1,024 instead of 1,000. The correct name for that unit is the gibibyte (GiB). One GiB is 1,073,741,824 bytes. The binary prefixes are defined in IEC 80000-13:2008.

Here is a 128 GB phone as an example:

  1. The maker counts decimal: 128 GB = 128,000,000,000 bytes.
  2. Divide by 1,073,741,824 to get GiB: 128,000,000,000 ÷ 1,073,741,824 ≈ 119.2 GiB.
  3. A tool that counts in GiB but labels it "GB" shows about 119 GB.

Nothing is missing. The same bytes are written in a different unit. Part of the space is also taken by Android itself and its system files, which is a real loss and separate from the unit difference.

To compare two numbers, make sure both use the same unit. Storage Analyzer uses decimal units, where 1 GB is 1,000 MB, so its sizes match the sizes on the box and the sizes in Android's storage settings.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units in one sum. Add 800 MB and 1.2 GB only after you convert: 800 MB = 0.8 GB, and 0.8 + 1.2 = 2 GB.
  • Dividing by 1,024 and calling the result GB. That gives GiB, which is a slightly larger unit. 1 GiB is about 1.074 GB.
  • Mixing up bits and bytes. A download speed in megabits per second (Mbit/s) is eight times smaller than the same number in megabytes per second (MB/s). File sizes use bytes.
  • Reading B as b. A capital B means byte. A lowercase b means bit.

Find out what takes up the space

Knowing the units helps you read the numbers. The harder part is finding the files that make them large. Storage Analyzer scans internal storage and the SD card when you tap Scan. It then shows:

  • A list of large files, sorted by size, with filters for images, videos, audio, documents, APKs and archives.
  • A map of folder sizes you can tap through.
  • Space used by each app, split into app, data and cache.

Files you delete go to the app's Trash first and stay there for 7 days, so a wrong tap can be undone.

Easy Onset Exercises for Stuttering, Step by Step

Easy onset means starting a word with a soft, gradual voice instead of a sudden push. It is a common technique in stuttering therapy, usually taught by a speech and language therapist. This page explains the idea, gives a short exercise ladder, and shows how a gentle start can be measured.

This is general information and not medical advice. A therapist can adjust the steps to you.

What easy onset is

When a voice starts abruptly, the vocal folds come together with a burst. In easy onset you let the voice fade in. You start the breath first, then add voice slowly, so the first sound rises like a ramp rather than a step.

An exercise ladder

Work through these in order. Move on when a step feels easy.

  1. Breath. Breathe in calmly, then let the air out slowly with no voice.
  2. Sigh. Let a soft "haaah" start from the breath and fade in.
  3. Vowel. Say "ahhh" and let the volume rise slowly over a second.
  4. Single words. Start vowel words such as "on", "easy" or "open" with the same fade-in.
  5. Short phrases. Join two or three words, keeping the first word soft.
  6. Full sentences. Start each sentence gently, then continue at a relaxed pace.

Keep your shoulders loose and don't force the first sound. Short sessions repeated often work better than one long session.

How to tell if the start was gentle

You can measure the start of a voice by watching its loudness. Stutter Practice uses this rule:

  1. Wait until the sound rises above a noise floor of −45 dB.
  2. Record loudness for the next 600 ms.
  3. Find the loudest point in that window.
  4. Find the first moment the sound came within 3 dB of that peak.
  5. The time from step 1 to step 4 is the rise time.
  6. A rise time of 120 ms or more counts as gentle. Less than 120 ms counts as too sudden.

After a reading, the meter waits until the sound has stayed below the floor for 300 ms before it listens for the next start.

Worked example

You say "on" and the meter sees these loudness values, with time counted from when the sound passed −45 dB:

TimeLevel
0 ms−44 dB
50 ms−36 dB
100 ms−28 dB
150 ms−22 dB
200 ms−20 dB
600 ms−20 dB

The peak is −20 dB. Within 3 dB of the peak means −23 dB or louder. The first value that qualifies is at 150 ms (−22 dB). The rise time is 150 ms, which is at least 120 ms, so the start is gentle.

If the same word had gone from −44 dB to −21 dB within 60 ms, the rise time would be 60 ms, and the start would be too sudden.

Common mistakes

  • Whispering instead of fading in voice. The goal is a soft start of normal voice.
  • Starting very slowly in every word of a long sentence. Practice the first word of each phrase, then speak on.
  • Holding tension in the neck or jaw.
  • Practicing in a noisy room. The meter reacts to any sound above the floor, so choose a quiet place.

Anti-Bucket List: No Journal

How to Keep a Journal of Things You Said No To

Most journals record what you did. A journal of declines records what you chose not to do. It helps you see your boundaries, your patterns and whether your no's hold up over time. This page shows what to write down, when to review it and how to read the results.

What to write down

Keep each entry short. You should be able to finish it in a minute, or you will stop doing it. Six fields are enough:

  1. What you declined. One line: "Weekend project for a colleague", "Phone upgrade", "Third evening event this week".
  2. Why. A sentence or two in your own words. Your future self will not remember your reasons.
  3. A reason tag. Pick the main one: time, money, energy, values or boundaries. Add your own tags if these don't fit.
  4. How it felt. Use a five-point scale: relieved, calm, neutral, hesitant, uneasy.
  5. The date you decided. This starts the review clock.
  6. What it saved, if you can tell. Optional: money and hours. Only write a number you believe.

Why the feeling matters

Write the feeling at the time of the decision, not later. A no that felt relieved and a no that felt uneasy are different data. Later you can compare: did the uneasy ones turn into regrets, or did you just need time to get used to them?

When to review

Don't review the same day. The urge to take it back, or the glow of relief, both fade. Review twice:

  • After 30 days. Enough time for the first doubts to show.
  • After 90 days. Enough time to see if the choice still stands.

For each review, answer one question: Am I still glad? Choose one of three answers:

  • Still glad
  • Unsure
  • Regret it

Add a short note if something specific changed.

If you change the decision date later, move both reviews by the same number of days.

How to read your journal

After a few months, look for patterns:

  • Which reason holds up? Count answers per tag. If most of your "energy" no's end as glad, that reason is reliable for you.
  • Where does regret show up? Divide regretted answers by all answered ones, per tag. Our regret rate calculator page shows how.
  • What did you save? Add up the money and hours you noted. Treat them as estimates, not accounting.
  • Do your first feelings predict the outcome? Compare the feeling you logged with the later answer.

Don't read too much into a handful of entries. Patterns need volume.

Tips that keep it going

  • Write the entry when you decline, while the details are fresh.
  • Keep reasons concrete: "I had no free evenings until May" beats "too busy".
  • Don't edit old reasons to look wiser. The point is an honest record.
  • Review even when the answer is "unsure". That is a valid result.
  • Keep the journal private if that helps you be honest.

Do it in the app

Anti-Bucket List is built for this routine. A quick-add form takes the fields above. After 30 and 90 days a local notification asks "Still glad?", and you answer glad, unsure or regret, with an optional note. If notifications are off, due check-ins show at the top of the journal.

You can:

  • search the titles and reasons you wrote
  • filter by tag
  • filter by check-in result: pending, due, glad, unsure or regret
  • see your totals and your answers on the Stats tab
  • add a home-screen widget with this month's no's
  • back up your journal to a JSON file and restore it later. The file is not encrypted, and the app warns you before each backup.

The app has no account and no internet permission, and it is excluded from Android cloud backup and device transfer. Pro is an optional one-time purchase through Google Play. It adds app lock, CSV export and advanced stats such as the regret rate per tag.

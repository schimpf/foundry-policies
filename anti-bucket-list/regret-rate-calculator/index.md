# Regret Rate Calculator: How Often Do You Regret No?

> Work out your regret rate for the things you declined. Formula, a worked example and a small calculator you can use right here.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/anti-bucket-list/regret-rate-calculator/
- App: Anti-Bucket List: No Journal, https://apps.schimpf.es/anti-bucket-list/index.md
If you say no on purpose, you can later work out how often you wished you had said yes. That share is your regret rate. This page shows the formula, a worked example and a small calculator. It also explains how to read the number.

## The formula

Look back at the decisions you have already reviewed. Sort each one into one of three answers:

- **Glad:** you still think saying no was right.
- **Unsure:** you can't tell yet.
- **Regret:** you wish you had said yes.

Then:

**Regret rate = regretted no's ÷ answered no's × 100**

"Answered" means all three groups added together: glad + unsure + regret. Unsure answers stay in the bottom of the fraction, so a pile of "unsure" pulls the rate down without claiming they were all fine.

Decisions you have not reviewed yet are left out. They have no answer, so they can't count for or against you.

## Worked example

Over six months you declined 15 things and reviewed 12 of them. The other 3 are not due yet.

| Answer | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Glad | 8 |
| Unsure | 3 |
| Regret | 1 |
| **Answered** | **12** |

1. Answered = 8 + 3 + 1 = 12
2. Regret rate = 1 ÷ 12 = 0.0833
3. As a percent: 0.0833 × 100 = 8.3%

The 3 pending no's are not part of the 12. When they are reviewed, the rate is worked out again.

## How to read the number

A single figure tells you little. Three things make it more useful:

- **Sample size.** One regret out of two answers is 50%, but that is two data points. Wait until you have around ten answers before you read much into it.
- **Per reason.** Work out the rate separately for each reason you gave: time, money, energy, values or boundaries. You may find that your no's about money almost never disappoint you, while no's about time sometimes do.
- **Unsure share.** If a lot of answers are "unsure", the check-in may be too early. Look at the same decision again later.

A rate of zero is not the goal. If you never regret a no, you may be saying no too safely. If you often do, your reasons may need another look. The number is a prompt for a question.

## When to ask: 30 and 90 days

A regret check works best when some time has passed. Anti-Bucket List asks twice, 30 days and 90 days after the date you decided. The first check catches the quick second thoughts. The second shows whether the feeling lasted.

If you answered twice for the same decision, the app uses the latest answer as the result for that entry. So an "unsure" at 30 days that becomes "glad" at 90 days counts as glad.

You can do the same on paper: write the date of each decision and add 30 and 90 days in your calendar.

## Keep the records in the app

Counting by hand works for a few decisions. It gets tedious after a few dozen. Anti-Bucket List is an offline journal for the things you said no to. Each entry has your reason, tags, how the no felt on a five-point scale from relieved to uneasy, and optional money and hours saved. After 30 and 90 days it asks "Still glad?" and you answer glad, unsure or regret, with an optional note.

You can filter your journal by result: pending, due, glad, unsure or regret. The Stats tab shows your answers as glad, unsure and regret. Pro adds the regret rate per tag, calculated the same way as above: regretted answers divided by answered ones, for the entries with that tag.

The app has no account and no internet permission. Your journal stays on your phone.

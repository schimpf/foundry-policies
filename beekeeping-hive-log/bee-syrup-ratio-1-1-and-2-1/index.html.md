> Sugar and water amounts for 1:1 and 2:1 bee syrup, with a chart, worked examples and metric to imperial conversions.

Bee syrup is sugar dissolved in water. The ratio tells you how much sugar to use for each part of water. This page gives the amounts for 1:1 and 2:1 syrup, worked examples, and the metric and imperial conversions.

## What the ratio means

The ratio is by weight. The first number is sugar and the second is water.

- 1:1 syrup is 1 part sugar to 1 part water, which is 50% sugar by weight.
- 2:1 syrup is 2 parts sugar to 1 part water, which is about 66.7% sugar by weight.

Water weighs about 1 g per ml, so 1 liter of water is about 1 kg. That makes the water side easy to measure with a jug.

Many beekeepers use the thinner 1:1 mix in spring and the thicker 2:1 mix in autumn. Check a beekeeping textbook or your local association for timing in your region.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: 2:1 syrup from 5 kg of sugar

- Water = sugar ÷ 2 = 5 kg ÷ 2 = 2.5 kg, about 2.5 L
- Total mix by weight: 5 + 2.5 = 7.5 kg

### Example 2: 1:1 syrup from 10 lb of sugar

- 1 oz is 28.3495 g, so 10 lb = 160 oz = about 4,536 g
- Water = same weight = about 4,536 g, which is about 4.54 L
- In US fluid ounces, 1 fl oz is 29.5735 ml, so 4,536 ml ÷ 29.5735 = about 153.4 fl oz

## Conversion factors

These are the factors used in Beekeeping Hive Log for feeding amounts.

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| 1 liter | 1,000 ml |
| 1 kilogram | 1,000 g |
| 1 US fluid ounce | 29.5735 ml |
| 1 ounce | 28.3495 g |

Rule of thumb: ml ÷ 29.5735 gives fluid ounces, and g ÷ 28.3495 gives ounces.

Example: 2 liters = 2,000 ml ÷ 29.5735 = 67.6 fl oz.

## Why weight and not cups

Sugar packs differently from one bag to the next, and a cup of sugar can hold very different amounts. Water is easy to measure because 1 ml weighs about 1 g. Weigh the sugar and measure the water by volume or weight. If you only have a volume measure for sugar, the ratio will drift, and your syrup may end up thinner or thicker than you planned.

Thin syrup (1:1) is about half sugar. Thick syrup (2:1) is about two thirds sugar, which is why it is heavier to stir and takes longer to dissolve. Warm water helps for 2:1.

## Mixing tips

1. Use plain white sugar. Do not use brown sugar or sweeteners.
2. Warm the water, not boiling hot, and stir until clear.
3. Let it cool before it goes in the feeder.
4. Weigh the sugar. Cup measures vary between people and sugar types.

## Logging feedings

Beekeeping Hive Log, on Google Play as "Hive Log: Beekeeping Journal", has a feeding log for each hive:

- Pick the type: syrup 1:1, syrup 2:1, fondant or pollen patty.
- Enter the amount. Syrups are liquids, so they use liters or fluid ounces. Fondant and pollen patty are solids, so they use kilograms or ounces.
- The app stores every amount in ml or g, and converts to your chosen unit system for display, with one decimal.
- Feedings appear on the hive timeline next to inspections and treatments.
- Data stays on your device. There is no account.

Switch between metric and imperial in Settings at any time. Stored amounts do not change.

Bee Treatment Withdrawal Period Calculator

Work out when a hive treatment withdrawal period ends. Formula, three worked examples, a quick table and a calculator.

A withdrawal period is the time you must wait after a treatment before you can use honey from that hive. This page shows how to work out the end date, with examples and a small calculator.

How to calculate the end date

The rule is addition.

End date = treatment date + withdrawal days

Beekeeping Hive Log uses exactly this rule. If the withdrawal is 0 days, the treatment has no withdrawal period and no end date is shown.

The number of days comes from the product label or from your national authority. Different products and countries give different numbers, and some products have none. Always read the label of the product you actually used.

Worked examples

Example 1: a 14-day withdrawal

  • Treatment date: 10 June
  • Withdrawal: 14 days
  • End date: 10 June + 14 days = 24 June

Until 24 June, treat honey from that hive as not usable.

Example 2: crossing a month end

  • Treatment date: 25 August
  • Withdrawal: 21 days
  • August has 31 days, so 25 August + 6 days = 31 August
  • 21 − 6 = 15 days left, so the end date is 15 September

Example 3: no withdrawal

  • Treatment date: 3 October
  • Withdrawal: 0 days
  • No end date. The treatment does not restrict honey use.

Two things people get wrong

  1. Counting the treatment day as day 1. The rule above starts counting the day after treatment. If your label counts differently, follow the label and add a day if in doubt.
  2. Several treatments on one hive. The hive is in a withdrawal period until the latest end date. An older treatment ending sooner does not clear it.

Why the end date matters

Honey taken during a withdrawal period may carry residues of the product. Beekeepers who sell or give away honey usually need to show when each hive was treated and when it was clear again. A written record with dates answers that question in seconds, and it also helps you plan: you can see which hives are clear before you put on supers or pull honey.

A simple routine

  1. Write down the product name and the date as soon as you treat.
  2. Copy the withdrawal days from the label into the same entry.
  3. Note the end date, or let the app work it out.
  4. Set a reminder for the end date so you know when the hive is clear.
  5. Check the date again before you harvest.

Warn, never block

A withdrawal date is a warning. You may need to log a late treatment or correct a date, so the app warns you while the period is running and still lets you save.

Keeping track in the app

Beekeeping Hive Log, on Google Play as "Hive Log: Beekeeping Journal", does this for each hive:

  • You log a treatment with the product, the date and the withdrawal days.
  • The hive shows an in-withdrawal status until the end date. If the hive also needs attention, for example an overdue reminder, that status takes priority.
  • The app can offer a reminder on the end date, as long as that date is still ahead.
  • It warns but never stops you from saving.
  • Everything is stored on your device. There is no account.

The date rule in the app is the same as in the calculator above, so the dates match.

Important note

This page explains date arithmetic only. It is not treatment advice. Follow your local regulations and the product label for treatments and withdrawal periods, and ask your local bee inspector or veterinarian if you are unsure.

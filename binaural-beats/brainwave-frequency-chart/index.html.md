# Brainwave Frequency Chart: Delta, Theta, Alpha, Beta, Gamma

> A chart of the five brainwave bands in Hz, what each is usually linked to, and which beat rates to use when you listen to binaural beats.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/binaural-beats/brainwave-frequency-chart/
- App: Binaural Beats, https://apps.schimpf.es/binaural-beats/index.md
Brainwave bands are ranges of frequency, measured in hertz (Hz), meaning cycles per second. Delta is the slowest and gamma the fastest. This page gives the chart, explains how to read it, and shows how the ranges map to beat rates in a beat generator.

## Brainwave frequency chart

| Band | Range (Hz) | Usually linked to |
|---|---|---|
| Delta (δ) | below 4 | Deep sleep |
| Theta (θ) | 4 to below 8 | Drowsiness, light sleep, daydreaming |
| Alpha (α) | 8 to below 12 | Relaxed wakefulness, eyes closed |
| Beta (β) | 12 to below 30 | Alert, active thinking |
| Gamma (γ) | 30 and above | Intense focus, fast processing |

Bands of this kind come from EEG research, the recording of electrical activity at the scalp. Hans Berger recorded the first human EEG in 1929 and described alpha and beta waves. Delta, theta and gamma were named later.

The exact edges differ between textbooks. Some start beta at 13 Hz, some end delta at 3 or 4 Hz, and some split alpha and beta into low and high parts. The table above uses the cut-offs of the Binaural Beats app, so it matches the band label the app shows.

## How to read the chart

- A band is a range of frequencies. Your brain produces activity in several bands at once.
- The labels are rough. "Alpha" is linked to relaxed wakefulness, but that does not mean 10 Hz sound will put you there.
- The chart describes recorded brain activity. It does not promise what a sound will do.

## From brainwave bands to beat rates

A beat is a slow pulse in loudness or in the difference between two tones. Its rate is also measured in Hz, so you can pick a beat rate that falls inside a band. A 10 Hz beat is an alpha-range beat. A 3 Hz beat is a delta-range beat.

Here is how the eight starting presets in the app fall into the bands:

| Preset | Beat | Band |
|---|---|---|
| Nap | 0.25 Hz | Delta |
| Deep Sleep | 2 Hz (starts at 8 Hz and glides down) | Delta |
| Wind Down | 3 Hz (starts at 12 Hz and glides down) | Delta |
| Meditation | 5 Hz | Theta |
| Relax | 6 Hz | Theta |
| Calm Focus | 10 Hz | Alpha |
| Active Focus | 15 Hz | Beta |
| Gamma Attention | 40 Hz | Gamma |

The Deep Sleep and Wind Down presets are made of steps. They pass through more than one band during a session. Wind Down, for example, starts at 12 Hz, which is already beta by the cut-offs above, and ends at 3 Hz.

## Which type of beat suits which rate

The type of beat changes what you hear. The band name stays the same.

- **Binaural beat:** each ear gets a slightly different pure tone. You perceive the difference as a slow pulse. This needs headphones. Beats above about 30 Hz are hard to hear this way.
- **Monaural beat:** both tones are mixed before they reach your ears, so the pulse is in the sound itself. Speakers work.
- **Isochronic beat:** a single tone that switches on and off at the beat rate.
- **Bilateral beat:** a single tone that moves between left and right once per beat cycle.

For fast beats such as 40 Hz, an isochronic or monaural beat is the more practical choice. That is why the Gamma Attention preset uses isochronic mode.

## What the research says

The evidence for beats is mixed. A 2019 meta-analysis, Garcia-Argibay, Santed and Reales, "Efficacy of binaural auditory beats in cognition, anxiety, and pain perception: a meta-analysis" (Psychological Research, 2019), pooled many small studies. It reported small to moderate effects, with anxiety among the clearer results, and large differences between studies.

The idea that the brain "follows" an outside rhythm, called entrainment, has not been shown consistently. Treat the chart as a way to choose a starting point and try what feels pleasant to you.

## Safety

Binaural beats are a relaxation tool. They do not treat any condition. Keep the volume low. Do not listen while driving or operating machinery. If you have epilepsy or a pacemaker, or any other medical condition, ask your doctor before using rhythmic sound or flashing visuals.

## Try the bands on your phone

Binaural Beats for Android generates all four beat types live on your phone. The beat runs from 0.25 to 45 Hz, and a label on the screen shows the band for the beat you set. Carrier tones run from 100 to 1000 Hz, with 200 to 500 Hz suggested as a comfortable range.

1. Open the app and choose a preset from the table above.
2. Move the beat slider and watch the band label change at 4, 8, 12 and 30 Hz.
3. Pick a session length from 5 to 90 minutes, or no limit.

Presets can be changed and saved as your own. The step editor lets you glide the beat from one rate to another over time, for example from 12 Hz down to 3 Hz, and shows the path on a chart. The app works offline and needs no account.

Dyslexia-Friendly Fonts Compared and How to Set Them Up

There is no single best font for every person with dyslexia. What the research and many readers agree on is that comfort comes from a mix: the letter shapes, the size and the spacing. This page compares three widely used fonts and gives starting values you can adjust.

The three fonts

FontMade byMain idea
LexendBonnie Shaver-Troup and Thomas Jockin, Lexend project (2018 onward)Wide, open letter shapes designed to improve reading fluency
OpenDyslexicAbelardo GonzalezHeavy bottoms on letters, so each letter looks anchored and harder to flip
Atkinson HyperlegibleBraille Institute, with Applied Design Works (2019)Letters that are easy to tell apart, such as I, l and 1, or O and 0

All three are released under the SIL Open Font License.

What the research says

  • Wery and Diliberto, "The effect of a specialized dyslexia font, OpenDyslexic, on reading rate and accuracy", *Annals of Dyslexia*, 2017: no significant gain in reading speed or accuracy over common fonts in the study group.
  • Rello and Baeza-Yates, "Good Fonts for Dyslexia", ACM ASSETS, 2013: sans serif, monospaced and roman fonts were read faster than italic ones, and readers preferred some fonts over others.
  • Zorzi and colleagues, "Extra-large letter spacing improves reading in dyslexia", *PNAS*, 2012: more space between letters helped children with dyslexia read faster and more accurately.

Take from this: spacing has the stronger evidence, and font choice is personal. Try each font on a page you know well and keep the one that feels easiest.

A starting point to try

  1. Pick a font from the list above.
  2. Raise the text size to 22 or 24 sp.
  3. Add 5 to 10% letter spacing.
  4. Add 20 to 30% word spacing so words do not run together.
  5. Set line height to 1.8 or 2.0.
  6. Read a page. Change one value at a time until it feels calm.

These numbers are a suggestion, not a medical rule. Your own comfort decides.

How to choose between them

  1. Pick one page of a book you know well.
  2. Read it in Lexend, then OpenDyslexic, then Atkinson Hyperlegible, with the same size and spacing.
  3. Note which one tires your eyes least after a few minutes.
  4. Check for letters that still swap, such as b and d, or p and q.
  5. Keep the font that feels easiest. Test again after a week.

A font that suits you today may not suit a different text or a different day. That is why the app keeps the font as a setting you can change at any time, not a fixed choice.

Colors and tint

Some readers find black text on a white page glaring. Soft backgrounds can help, though the evidence is mixed, so test it yourself. In the app, reading presets store background color, text color, font and spacing together, so you can switch between a day setup and a night setup in one tap. Light and dark mode follow your system setting.

Keep your place

Even with a good font, it is easy to jump a line. A line focus ruler dims the lines around the one you are reading.

  • The basic ruler covers two lines and dims the rest by 50%.
  • The adjustable ruler can be 1 to 5 lines tall, dim from 0 to 90%, and add a yellow, blue or rose tint.

# BMI Calculator and Chart: Formula in kg/cm and lb/in

> How to calculate BMI in kg and cm or lb and inches, with the WHO category chart, a worked example and a calculator. For adults only.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/glp1/bmi-calculator-kg-cm-lb-in/
- App: GLP Mate: GLP-1 Shot Tracker, https://apps.schimpf.es/glp1/index.md
Body mass index (BMI) compares your weight to your height. This page shows the formula in metric and imperial units, the standard WHO categories, a worked example and a small calculator.

BMI is a screening number. It does not diagnose anything and does not measure body fat or muscle. Talk to your doctor about what it means for you.

## The formula

**Metric:** BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in m × height in m)

**Imperial:** BMI = 703 × weight in lb ÷ (height in inches × height in inches)

Height in meters is your height in centimeters divided by 100.

## Worked example

You weigh 92 kg and are 175 cm tall.

1. Height in meters: 175 ÷ 100 = 1.75.
2. Square it: 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625.
3. Divide: 92 ÷ 3.0625 = 30.04.
4. BMI ≈ 30.0.

The same person in imperial units: 202.8 lb and 68.9 inches. 703 × 202.8 ÷ (68.9 × 68.9) = 142,568 ÷ 4,747 ≈ 30.0, the same result.

Another example: 70 kg and 170 cm. 1.70 × 1.70 = 2.89. 70 ÷ 2.89 = 24.2.

## BMI chart for adults

Source: World Health Organization, "Obesity: preventing and managing the global epidemic", WHO Technical Report Series 894 (2000). The same cut-offs appear in the WHO BMI classification for adults aged 18 and older.

| BMI (kg/m²) | WHO category |
|---|---|
| below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 to 24.9 | Normal range |
| 25.0 to 29.9 | Overweight (pre-obese) |
| 30.0 to 34.9 | Obesity class I |
| 35.0 to 39.9 | Obesity class II |
| 40.0 and above | Obesity class III |

Limits to keep in mind:

- The table is for adults. Children and teenagers use age- and sex-specific growth charts.
- A WHO expert consultation (published in The Lancet, 2004, "Appropriate body-mass index for Asian populations and its implications for policy and intervention strategies") found that health risks rise at lower BMI values in many Asian populations. Some countries use lower cut-offs.
- Very muscular people can have a high BMI with little body fat. Pregnancy and some illnesses also change the picture.

## Weight needed for a given BMI

Rearrange the formula to find a weight for your height:

**weight (kg) = BMI × height in m × height in m**

For a height of 1.75 m (3.0625 m²):

| Target BMI | Weight |
|---|---|
| 18.5 | 56.7 kg |
| 25 | 76.6 kg |
| 30 | 91.9 kg |

## Use a trend, not a single weigh-in

Body weight moves by a kilogram or more from day to day because of water, salt, food and the time of day. A single BMI value calculated from one weigh-in can mislead you.

A 7-day average helps. Add up the weights of the last 7 days and divide by the number of days with a reading. It smooths daily noise, so you see the direction over time.

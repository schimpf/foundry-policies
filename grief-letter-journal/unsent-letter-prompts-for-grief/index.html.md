# Unsent Letter Prompts for Grief, by Relationship

> Gentle prompts for an unsent letter to a parent, partner, child, sibling, friend or pet you lost, plus simple steps to start writing.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/grief-letter-journal/unsent-letter-prompts-for-grief/
- App: Grief Letter Journal, https://apps.schimpf.es/grief-letter-journal/index.md
An unsent letter is a letter you write to someone who has died, and do not send. It gives you a place to say what you did not get to say. There is no reader, so there is nothing to get wrong.

This page gives a simple way to begin, then prompts for each kind of relationship.

## How to write an unsent letter

1. Pick a quiet time. A few minutes is enough. Stop when you want to.
2. Start with "Dear" and their name. A first line makes the blank page easier.
3. Write to them and use "you". Say it the way you would have said it.
4. Choose one prompt, or none. A prompt is optional. Use it or skip it.
5. Do not edit. Spelling, order and tone do not matter here.
6. Decide how it ends. You can keep the letter, put it away sealed, or let it go.

There is no right length. One line is a letter.

## Prompts for any person

- What happened today that you wish you could tell them?
- What do you wish you had said the last time you spoke?
- What would you like to thank them for?
- Which small, ordinary moment with them do you return to most?
- Is there something you have never said out loud?

## Prompts for a parent

- What is something they taught you that you still do without thinking?
- Which meal, smell or song brings them back to you?

## Prompts for a partner

- What did your mornings together look like?
- What plans did you make together that you still think about?

## Prompts for a child

- What made you proud of them?
- What do you imagine telling them about your day?

## Prompts for a sibling

- Which childhood memory with them makes you smile?
- What only the two of you understood?

## Prompts for a friend

- When did they make you laugh the hardest?
- What would you call to tell them first?

## Prompts for a pet

The loss of a pet is a real loss. These prompts are written for animals.

- Which part of your daily routine still feels like theirs?
- How did they greet you when you came home?
- Where was their favorite spot to rest?
- What did they teach you about patience or joy?
- What is the funniest trouble they ever got into?

## Three ways to end a letter

Some people want to keep what they wrote. Others want to let it go. Choose the ending that fits the day. You can choose differently next time.

- Keep. The letter stays where you can read it and add to it.
- Seal. You stop editing, and the letter becomes read-only. You can open it again later.
- Release. You let the letter go for good. It is deleted and cannot be brought back.

Releasing on paper might mean burning or burying the page. Do this only if it is safe, and only if you are sure.

## Tips if you get stuck

- Write one sentence that begins "I miss…" and see where it goes.
- Write about something small, like a sound, a place or a habit.
- Write what you would tell them about today.
- Come back another day.
- If writing brings up more than you can hold, stop. Talk to someone you trust, or to a local support service.

Writing is not a replacement for professional support.

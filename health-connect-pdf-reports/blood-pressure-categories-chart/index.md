# Blood Pressure Categories Chart (AHA, mmHg)

> AHA blood pressure chart with the five categories, the exact mmHg limits, how "or" rules work, and worked examples for home readings.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/health-connect-pdf-reports/blood-pressure-categories-chart/
- App: Health Reports: BP & Sleep PDF, https://apps.schimpf.es/health-connect-pdf-reports/index.md
A blood pressure reading has two numbers. The top number is systolic pressure, when the heart beats. The bottom number is diastolic pressure, when the heart rests between beats. Both are written in millimeters of mercury (mmHg), for example 124/78.

This page shows the American Heart Association (AHA) categories for adults, explains how to place a reading when the two numbers fall in different rows, and walks through examples.

## Blood pressure chart for adults

| Category | Systolic (top) | | Diastolic (bottom) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | below 120 | and | below 80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and | below 80 |
| Stage 1 | 130–139 | or | 80–89 |
| Stage 2 | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher |
| Above 180/120 (the AHA calls this a hypertensive crisis) | above 180 | and/or | above 120 |

Source: Whelton PK, et al. *2017 ACC/AHA/AAPA/ABC/ACPM/AGS/APhA/ASH/ASPC/NMA/PCNA Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure in Adults*. Hypertension, 2018; 71(6): e13–e115. The chart applies to adults only.

## How to read the chart

The words "and" and "or" matter.

- Normal needs both numbers to be low: systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80.
- Elevated only exists for the top number. Systolic is 120–129 while diastolic stays below 80.
- Stage 1 and Stage 2 use "or". If either number reaches the range, the reading is in that stage.
- Above 180/120 also uses "or". One number past the limit is enough.

When the two numbers point to different rows, the higher category wins.

### The rule step by step

Check from the top of the danger scale down. Stop at the first rule that matches.

1. Systolic above 180 or diastolic above 120: Above 180/120.
2. Systolic 140 or higher, or diastolic 90 or higher: Stage 2.
3. Systolic 130 or higher, or diastolic 80 or higher: Stage 1.
4. Systolic 120 or higher: Elevated.
5. Anything else: Normal.

Rule 4 only needs the systolic check because a diastolic of 80 or more was already caught by rule 3.

## Worked examples

| Reading | What decides it | Category |
|---|---|---|
| 118/76 | Both numbers are low | Normal |
| 124/78 | Systolic is 120–129, diastolic is below 80 | Elevated |
| 124/82 | Diastolic 82 is in the 80–89 row, which beats Elevated | Stage 1 |
| 134/78 | Systolic 134 is in the 130–139 row | Stage 1 |
| 128/92 | Diastolic 92 is 90 or higher | Stage 2 |
| 142/84 | Systolic 142 is 140 or higher | Stage 2 |
| 185/95 | Systolic is above 180 | Above 180/120 |
| 150/125 | Diastolic is above 120 | Above 180/120 |

Two edge cases:

- 120/70 is Elevated. Normal means below 120, so 120 itself is already in the next row.
- 180/120 exactly is Stage 2. The top row begins above 180 or above 120, so 180 and 120 do not count as past the limit.

## One reading is not a result

A single home reading can swing with stress, caffeine, a full bladder, a recent walk or the cuff size. The guideline bases its categories on the average of two or more readings taken on two or more occasions. That is why a doctor usually asks for a log of several days or weeks.

Good habits when you log readings:

- Sit quietly for five minutes before measuring.
- Keep your back supported and your feet flat on the floor.
- Rest your arm at heart level.
- Measure at similar times each day.
- Write down every reading, not only the ones that look good.

## What the chart does not tell you

The chart sorts numbers into groups. What a group means for you depends on age, other conditions, medicines and symptoms, and only a doctor can weigh them. If a reading is above 180/120, or you feel unwell with a high reading, contact a doctor or emergency service according to local guidance.

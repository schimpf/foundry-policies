Horse Farrier Schedule Calculator: Next Due Date

Booking the farrier is easy. Remembering when the next visit is due, for every horse and every kind of care, is the hard part. This page shows how to work out the next due date from the last visit, with a calculator, a worked example and a table of common intervals.

The same method works for worming, vaccines, dentist visits or anything else that repeats.

Calculate the next due date

Enter the date of the last visit and how often it repeats.



The formula

The rule is simple:

next due date = date of last visit + interval in days

Intervals are counted in days, so they are easy to check by hand:

  • 1 week = 7 days
  • 1 month = 30 days
  • 1 year = 365 days

A month counts as a flat 30 days, not a calendar month. "3 months" is 90 days. This keeps every interval the same length, so a schedule does not drift with short or long months. If your farrier works to calendar months, use weeks instead. Weeks are exact.

Worked example

Your horse had the farrier on 3 November 2026, and your farrier works on a 6-week cycle.

  1. Convert the interval to days: 6 weeks × 7 = 42 days.
  2. Add 42 days to 3 November. November has 30 days, so 27 days gets you to 30 November. The remaining 15 days land on 15 December 2026.
  3. If you want a heads-up three days earlier, subtract 3 days: 12 December 2026.

Which date should you count from?

Count from the day the work was done, not the day it was booked. If the visit slips a week, the whole cycle moves with it. That is what you want. The interval is the gap between visits, not a fixed day on the calendar.

Two cases to watch:

  • A late visit. The next date moves later too. If the farrier is a week late, the next visit is due a week later than a fixed calendar plan would say.
  • An early visit. The next date is counted from the early visit. The gap between visits stays the same length.

Overdue, this week and later

Once you have a due date, sort each item by how close it is:

  • Overdue: the due date is before today.
  • This week: the due date is today or within the next 7 days.
  • Later: more than 7 days away.

With several horses this sorting matters most. A single list of overdue and this-week items tells you what to book first.

Do it on paper or in a spreadsheet

You can keep this by hand:

  1. Write the last visit date for each horse and each type of care.
  2. Write the interval in days next to it.
  3. Add the two to get the next due date.
  4. Re-do the sum every time you log a visit.

In a spreadsheet, put the last date in one cell and the days in another. Then the next due date is the sum of the two cells, formatted as a date.

Do it in the app

Horse Care Log does this sum for you. It is an Android app that works offline, with no account.

  • You set a schedule once: the type (farrier, worming, vaccine or custom), the interval and the next due date. New schedules start with typical intervals: 6 weeks for farrier, 12 weeks for worming and 1 year for vaccines. You can change them.
  • One tap on "Log done" records the visit for today and sets the next due date to today plus the interval. If you tapped by mistake, undo it.
  • A reminder arrives 3 days before the due date and another on the day. Lock screen reminders do not show horse names.
  • The Today screen lists what is overdue and due this week across all your horses. A home screen widget shows the same.

The app uses the same formula as the calculator above. Search for Horse Care Log on Google Play to try it.

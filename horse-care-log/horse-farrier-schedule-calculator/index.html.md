# Horse Farrier Schedule Calculator: Next Due Date

> Work out when your horse's next farrier, worming or vaccine date falls from the last visit and the interval, with a worked example and a table.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/horse-care-log/horse-farrier-schedule-calculator/
- App: Horse Care Log: Farrier & Vet, https://apps.schimpf.es/horse-care-log/index.md
Remembering when the next farrier visit is due, for every horse and every kind of care, takes more effort than booking it. This page shows how to work out the next due date from the last visit, with a calculator, a worked example and a table of common intervals.

The same method works for worming, vaccines, dentist visits or anything else that repeats.

## Calculate the next due date

Enter the date of the last visit and how often it repeats.

An interactive calculator is on the web page: https://apps.schimpf.es/horse-care-log/horse-farrier-schedule-calculator/

## The formula

The rule is:

**next due date = date of last visit + interval in days**

Intervals are counted in days, so they are easy to check by hand:

- 1 week = 7 days
- 1 month = 30 days
- 1 year = 365 days

A month always counts as 30 days, whatever the calendar says. "3 months" is 90 days. This keeps every interval the same length, so a schedule does not drift with short or long months. If your farrier works to calendar months, use weeks instead. Weeks are exact.

## Worked example

Your horse had the farrier on 3 November 2026, and your farrier works on a 6-week cycle.

1. Convert the interval to days: 6 weeks × 7 = 42 days.
2. Add 42 days to 3 November. November has 30 days, so 27 days gets you to 30 November. The remaining 15 days land on 15 December 2026.
3. If you want a heads-up three days earlier, subtract 3 days to get 12 December 2026.

The same last-visit date with other intervals:

| Care | Interval | Days | Next due |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farrier | 6 weeks | 42 | 15 December 2026 |
| Worming | 12 weeks | 84 | 26 January 2027 |
| Quarterly check | 3 months | 90 | 1 February 2027 |
| Yearly vaccine | 1 year | 365 | 3 November 2027 |

## Common farrier intervals in weeks

Your farrier decides the right interval for your horse. Horses differ, and the interval can change with the season, the workload and the state of the hoof. Use the table to convert a number of weeks into days:

| Weeks | Days |
|---|---|
| 4 | 28 |
| 5 | 35 |
| 6 | 42 |
| 7 | 49 |
| 8 | 56 |

## Which date should you count from?

Count from the day the work was done. If the visit slips a week, the whole cycle moves with it. The interval is the gap between visits.

Two cases to watch:

- **A late visit.** The next date moves later too. If the farrier is a week late, the next visit is due a week later than a fixed calendar plan would say.
- **An early visit.** The next date is counted from the early visit. The gap between visits stays the same length.

## Overdue, this week and later

Once you have a due date, sort each item by how close it is:

- **Overdue:** the due date is before today.
- **This week:** the due date is today or within the next 7 days.
- **Later:** more than 7 days away.

With several horses this sorting matters most. A single list of overdue and this-week items tells you what to book first.

## Do it on paper or in a spreadsheet

You can keep this by hand:

1. Write the last visit date for each horse and each type of care.
2. Write the interval in days next to it.
3. Add the two to get the next due date.
4. Re-do the sum every time you log a visit.

In a spreadsheet, put the last date in one cell and the days in another. Then the next due date is the sum of the two cells, formatted as a date.

Horse Vaccination Record: What to Write Down

A horse vaccination record is the paper trail that shows what your horse had, when, and when the next dose is due. Shows, livery yards, transport companies and new vets often ask for it. A good record takes a minute to fill in at each visit and saves a lot of searching later.

This page lists what to write down, gives a template you can copy and shows how to work out the next due date.

What a vaccination record should contain

Write one line per vaccination. Each line needs these five things:

  1. Date. The day the vaccine was given.
  2. Product. The name on the vial or the vet's invoice, for example the brand and what it protects against.
  3. Batch number. The lot number printed on the vial. If a product is ever recalled, the batch number tells you whether your horse is affected.
  4. Vet. The name of the vet or practice that gave it.
  5. Next due date. The date of the next dose, so you can plan ahead.

Also keep, once per horse:

  • Name, breed, birth date, sex and colour
  • Microchip number and passport number

Work out the next due date

The next due date is the date given plus the interval your vet gives you:

next due = date given + interval in days

Counting in days keeps it exact:

  • 1 week = 7 days
  • 1 year = 365 days

Worked example

Your horse had a yearly booster on 3 November 2026. The interval is 1 year, which is 365 days.

  1. Take the date given: 3 November 2026.
  2. Add 365 days.
  3. The next dose is due on 3 November 2027.

A year of 365 days can end a day earlier than the calendar date in a leap year. If the date matters, for example for a show entry deadline, check it against the vet's plan and use the earlier date when in doubt.

If the vet asks for a booster after a shorter gap, such as 4 weeks, use the same sum: 4 × 7 = 28 days.

The interval comes from your vet

How often a horse needs each vaccine depends on the product, the horse's history, where you live and what rules apply to shows or competitions. This page does not set an interval. Follow the product's data sheet and your vet's advice, and the rules of your national federation or event if you compete.

Vaccination guidelines are published by veterinary bodies. In the United States, the American Association of Equine Practitioners (AAEP) publishes its Vaccination Guidelines, which list the core vaccines and how a vet decides on risk-based ones. Your own vet knows which apply where you live.

Common mistakes

  • Missing batch numbers. Take a photo of the vial label or ask the vet to write it down before they leave.
  • Writing the interval, not the date. "Yearly" does not help in two years' time. Write the date.
  • Records on loose paper. Keep everything for one horse in one place.
  • No copy. If the only record is the passport or one notebook, a loss means starting from nothing.

TDS on Contractor Payment 194C: Calculator and Example

Section 194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 covers payments to a contractor or sub-contractor for carrying out work. In the Income-tax Act 2025 the same rule sits at section 393(1), serial 6. This page shows the rate, the limits that decide whether you deduct, and a worked example.

The 194C rate

PayeeWith PANWithout PAN
Individual or HUF1%20%
Anyone else (company, firm, and so on)2%20%

These are the rates used in India Offline TDS Section Calc for FY 2025-26. Without a PAN the rate is 20%, whatever the payee type.

When 194C applies

TDS is due if either of these is true:

  1. A single payment is more than ₹30,000.
  2. Payments to the same contractor in the financial year add up to more than ₹1,00,000.

Both tests use "more than". A payment of exactly ₹30,000 does not trigger the first test.

The second test matters. A contractor paid ₹25,000 four times in a year never crosses ₹30,000 in one payment. The fourth payment takes the total to ₹1,00,000, which is not more than the limit. A fifth payment crosses it, and TDS applies to that payment.

Notes that change the base or the outcome:

  • Deduct at the time of credit to the contractor's account or at payment, whichever is earlier.
  • If GST is shown separately on the invoice, deduct on the amount without GST.
  • A transporter who owns 10 or fewer goods carriages and gives a PAN declaration has no TDS deducted.
  • Payments for personal purposes by an individual or HUF are not covered.

The formula

TDS = taxable amount × rate, rounded half-up to the whole rupee.

Net payable = payment − TDS.

When a payment crosses the yearly limit, the app deducts on the whole current payment, not only the part above the limit.

Worked examples

Example 1: company, with PAN

You pay a company ₹45,000 for a repair job. The amount is above ₹30,000, so TDS applies.

  • Rate: 2%
  • TDS: 45,000 × 2% = ₹900
  • Net payable: 45,000 − 900 = ₹44,100

Example 2: individual, yearly limit crossed

You have paid an individual contractor ₹80,000 this year. Now you pay ₹25,000.

  • Single payment: ₹25,000 is not above ₹30,000.
  • Yearly total: 80,000 + 25,000 = ₹1,05,000, above ₹1,00,000. TDS applies.
  • Rate: 1%
  • TDS: 25,000 × 1% = ₹250
  • Net payable: ₹24,750

Example 3: no PAN

You pay an individual ₹45,000 and they give no PAN.

  • Rate: 20%
  • TDS: 45,000 × 20% = ₹9,000
  • Net payable: ₹36,000

Deposit and filing

Deposit TDS by the 7th of the following month. For TDS deducted in March, the date is 30 April. Report non-salary TDS in Form 26Q each quarter. The due dates are 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 31 May.

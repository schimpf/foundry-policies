How to read the chart

Rate

The rate is a percentage of the taxable amount. The app rounds the result half-up to the whole rupee. For example, 2% of ₹45,000 is ₹900.

No-PAN rate

If the payee gives no PAN, a higher rate applies. For most sections it is 20%. For purchase of goods (194Q) and e-commerce (194-O) it is 5%. Winnings under 194B and 194BB are already at 30%, so the rate does not change.

Threshold

Below the threshold, no TDS is deducted. The threshold is measured in one of three ways:

  • Per payment: each payment is tested alone (194B, 194BB, 194-IA, 194EE).
  • Per month: payments in the same month are added up (rent under 194-I and 194-IB).
  • Per financial year: payments to the same payee are added up over the year (most other sections).

Some limits count whether the amount is "more than" the limit or "at least" the limit. Property transfer (194-IA) and NSS payments (194EE) apply at the limit itself. For the others, the amount must exceed the limit.

Excess-only sections

Sections 194Q and 194N tax only the part above the limit. If you have paid a seller ₹48 lakh and now pay ₹10 lakh more, only ₹8 lakh is taxable under 194Q. At 0.1% that is ₹800.

Which Act's numbers to use

The Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the Income-tax Act 1961 for tax years starting on or after 1 April 2026. For FY 2025-26 payments, the 1961 Act section numbers are the ones in force. The 2025 Act numbers in the chart help you move your records and templates to the new numbering.

Check the notes before you deduct

A chart cannot hold every condition. A few examples:

  • On 194C, no TDS applies to a transporter with up to 10 goods carriages who gives a PAN declaration.
  • If GST is shown separately on the invoice, TDS on contractor and rent payments is on the amount without GST.
  • 194Q applies only if the buyer's turnover in the previous year was above ₹10 crore.
  • Senior citizen interest at ₹1,00,000 applies to interest from a bank or post office.

COBRA Election Deadline: How the 60 Days Work

How long you have to elect COBRA after a layoff, when the 60 days start, how to count them and which other health coverage dates to check.

If your employer health plan ends with your job, COBRA lets you keep it, usually at your own cost. You have to choose within a set window. This page explains how the 60-day window works, how to count it and what else to check.

The short answer

  • You have at least 60 days to elect COBRA coverage.
  • The 60 days start on the later of two dates: the day you lose coverage, or the day you receive your election notice.
  • If you elect in time, coverage is back-dated to the day your old coverage ended.
  • Missing the window usually means losing the right to COBRA.

Source: U.S. Department of Labor, Employee Benefits Security Administration, *An Employee's Guide to Health Benefits Under COBRA* (2020 edition), and 29 CFR 2590.606-4.

Who it applies to

COBRA applies to group health plans of employers with 20 or more employees. Losing a job, other than for gross misconduct, is a qualifying event. Many states have similar "mini-COBRA" laws for smaller employers. Rules differ by state, so check yours.

The notice chain

The 60 days often start later than your last day. The steps are:

  1. Your employer tells the plan administrator about the job loss, within 30 days.
  2. The plan administrator sends you an election notice, within 14 days after that. Where the employer is also the administrator, the total is 44 days.
  3. Your 60-day window starts, counted from the later of the notice date or the date coverage ended.

Source: the same DOL guide, and ERISA section 606.

Keep the envelope or email the notice came in. The date you received it can matter.

How to count 60 days

Count the day after the start date as day 1. A simple way: add 60 days to the start date.

Coverage ended or notice receivedElection deadline
January 31April 1 (non-leap year)
March 15May 14
June 30August 29
October 10December 9

Check the date printed in your notice and follow it.

What you pay and for how long

  • Cost: you usually pay the full premium, plus up to 2% for administration. Your employer no longer pays its share.
  • First payment: due 45 days after you elect. Later payments follow the plan's schedule, usually monthly.
  • Length: up to 18 months after a job loss.

Source: the DOL guide named above.

You do not have to decide on day one

Because coverage is back-dated, some people wait. If you have no medical bills in the gap, you can sometimes wait until the end of the window to see if you need it. If you get sick in that gap and have not yet elected, coverage is only there if you elect in time and pay. Do not rely on this without reading your notice.

COBRA is not your only option

Losing job-based coverage also opens a Special Enrollment Period on the Health Insurance Marketplace. You generally have 60 days from the loss of coverage to pick a plan, and in many cases up to 60 days before it. Source: HealthCare.gov, "Special Enrollment Period" (healthcare.gov).

Compare:

  • COBRA premium for the same plan you have now
  • Marketplace plans, which may come with subsidies based on income
  • A spouse or partner's employer plan, which has its own enrollment window

Use your expected income, which is likely lower than last year, when you check subsidies.

Other dates to write down

COBRA is one of several dates that come after a layoff. In the Job Loss 12-Week Plan app, the defaults, counted from your layoff date, are:

DeadlineDefault
Unemployment filing7 days after layoff
Final pay14 days after layoff
401(k) rollover review30 days after layoff
Health coverage endsLast day of the layoff month
COBRA election60 days after layoff

These are generic starting points. State law sets final pay timing, and your plan sets when coverage ends. Change each date to match your paperwork.

Using the 60-day count

  1. Find your coverage end date. Ask HR, or look at your last pay stub or benefits portal.
  2. Ask HR for your COBRA notice and when it was sent.
  3. Add 60 days to the later of those two dates.
  4. Put the result in your calendar with a reminder at least a week earlier.
  5. Compare COBRA and Marketplace costs before the deadline arrives.

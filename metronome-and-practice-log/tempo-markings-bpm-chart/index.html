Tempo Markings BPM Chart: Largo to Prestissimo

Italian tempo markings such as Allegro and Andante tell you how fast to play. A metronome tells you in beats per minute (BPM). This page connects the two with a chart you can use at the music stand.

Tempo markings and BPM ranges

The ranges below are the ones used by the app Metronome & Practice Log, which shows the marking next to the BPM you set. The app's metronome runs from 30 to 300 BPM.

MarkingBPM rangeFeel
Largo30-59Very slow, broad
Adagio60-75Slow, calm
Andante76-107Walking pace
Moderato108-119Moderate
Allegro120-167Fast, lively
Presto168-199Very fast
Prestissimo200-300As fast as possible

Each range starts at its lower number. Andante covers 76 up to and including 107. At 108 the marking changes to Moderato.

These ranges are a guide, not a law

Composers and publishers did not agree on exact numbers. Different books and metronome makers list slightly different ranges, and the edges of each marking overlap in practice. Treat the chart as a starting point.

  • A marking describes character as well as speed. An Allegro can feel relaxed at 120 BPM and urgent at 160.
  • The beat the BPM counts matters. A tempo of 60 BPM counts quarter notes in most 4/4 music, but a score may count half notes or dotted quarters instead.
  • If the score gives a number, such as quarter note = 96, use the number and ignore the word.

How to find the BPM for a piece

  1. Check the score for a metronome mark at the top, usually a note symbol followed by "= number".
  2. If there is only an Italian word, pick a BPM inside its range. Start in the middle or a little below.
  3. Play a short phrase. If the fastest passage falls apart, lower the BPM by 4 to 8.
  4. Write down the number that works, so next time you start from the same place.

Setting the tempo in the app

  • Use the minus and plus buttons for single steps.
  • Use the slider for big jumps.
  • Use tap tempo to match a recording or another player. The app takes the median of the last four taps, which ignores one sloppy tap.
  • The marking name updates as you cross each boundary in the chart.

Practicing with markings

A common way to build speed is to start slower than the marking, play the passage cleanly, and raise the tempo in small steps over several days. For example, a passage marked Allegro at 132 BPM might start at 96 BPM (Andante) and move up by 4 BPM each day.

Reading the chart with a time signature

The marking tells you the pace of the beat, and the BPM counts that beat. In 4/4 and 3/4 the beat is normally a quarter note, so Andante at 90 BPM means 90 quarter notes per minute. In 6/8 many players count two dotted-quarter pulses per bar, so a slow 6/8 may sit at 50 to 70 BPM, while counting every eighth note would give 150 to 210 BPM for the same music.

The app clicks once per beat of the bar you choose. It offers 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4 and 6/8, and in 6/8 it accents the first and fourth click by default. Set the BPM to match the way you count.

Quick reference

  • Slower than 60 BPM: Largo.
  • 60 to 75 BPM: Adagio.
  • 76 to 107 BPM: Andante.
  • 108 to 119 BPM: Moderato.
  • 120 to 167 BPM: Allegro.
  • 168 to 199 BPM: Presto.
  • 200 BPM and above: Prestissimo.

Spain Mortgage Early Repayment Fee: Legal Caps

Maximum early repayment fee on a Spanish mortgage under Ley 5/2019, by rate type and year, with a worked example and how to compare your options.

If you pay extra on a Spanish mortgage, the bank may charge an early repayment fee (comisión por amortización anticipada). Since 2019 the law limits how much. This page gives the caps, a worked example, and how to decide whether paying early is worth it.

The source

The caps come from Ley 5/2019, de 15 de marzo, reguladora de los contratos de crédito inmobiliario (the Spanish mortgage credit law). It applies to loans signed after 16 June 2019. Variable-rate loans are covered by article 23 and fixed-rate loans by article 25. Check your own contract: the fee written there is the most the bank can charge, and it can be lower.

The caps as a table

The fee is a percentage of the amount you repay early, not of the whole loan.

Rate typePeriod counted from signingMaximum fee
FixedMonths 1 to 120 (first 10 years)2.00 %
FixedAfter month 1201.50 %
VariableFirst 3 years0.25 %
VariableAfter 3 years0 %

For variable loans, the law lets the contract choose one of two options. The second is 0.15 % during the first 5 years, then nothing. Hipoteca uses the 3-year, 0.25 % option.

Mixed loans

A mixed mortgage has a fixed period and then a variable one. Each month follows the rule for the type that applies in that month:

  • During the fixed period, the fixed caps apply.
  • After it ends, the variable caps apply, still counted from signing.

So a mixed loan whose fixed period lasts 5 years has a 2 % cap in years 1 to 5. After year 5, the 3-year variable window has already passed, so the fee is 0 %.

Worked example

You pay an extra 20,000 euros.

LoanWhenFee
FixedYear 420,000 × 2.00 % = 400 euros
FixedYear 1220,000 × 1.50 % = 300 euros
VariableYear 220,000 × 0.25 % = 50 euros
VariableYear 40 euros

On a fixed loan, waiting past month 120 cuts the cap from 2.00 % to 1.50 %. On a variable loan, waiting past month 36 removes the fee.

Reduce the term or reduce the payment?

When you repay early you choose what the saving does:

  1. Reduce the term. The payment stays the same and the loan ends sooner. This saves the most interest.
  2. Reduce the payment. The term stays the same and the monthly payment falls. This gives you more room each month.

After either choice the bank recalculates with the same annuity formula, using the new balance. A payment of 20,000 euros early saves interest on that amount for every remaining month, so early repayments pay off most when many years remain.

Is it worth paying the fee?

Compare the fee with the interest you avoid.

  • Fixed loan at 2.60 % with 20 years left: 20,000 euros of debt costs about 5,700 euros in interest over those 20 years, far more than a 300 euro fee.
  • A low rate and a short time left shrink the saving, so the same fee may not pay back.
  • If you can invest the money at a higher return than your mortgage rate, repaying may not be the best use.
  • Keep an emergency reserve. Money you repay cannot be borrowed back.

Things to check

  • The date of signing: the windows count from there, not from today.
  • Whether your loan predates June 2019. Older contracts follow their own terms.
  • Whether the bank charges the fee on the amount repaid or on something else. It must be on the amount repaid.

An IBS food diary is only useful if you can read it. The simple question is whether your symptoms run higher than usual after you eat a certain food. This page shows a plain way to answer it with arithmetic, with a worked example and a calculator.

## The method in four steps

You need symptom scores on a 0 to 10 scale (0 none, 10 worst), logged several times a day, and a note of what you ate and when.

1. Baseline. Average every entry you logged (if an entry rates several symptoms, use their mean for that entry). This is your usual level.
2. Window. Choose how long after eating to look. Two to six hours after the meal is a common starting point.
3. Per meal. For each meal with a food tag, average the entries logged inside the window. A meal with no scores in its window is skipped.
4. Per tag. Average those per-meal values for each tag. Subtract the baseline. A positive difference means symptoms ran higher than usual after that food.

Each meal is averaged first so that a meal followed by five quick entries does not count five times. Each meal counts once.

## Worked example

Baseline from all entries in the last month: 3.2.

You tagged "onion" on four meals. Window: 2 to 6 hours after eating.

| Meal | Scores in the window | Meal average |
|------|----------------------|--------------|
| Monday dinner | 5, 7 | 6.0 |
| Wednesday lunch | 4 | 4.0 |
| Friday dinner | 6, 6, 3 | 5.0 |
| Sunday lunch | 7 | 7.0 |

- Onion average: (6.0 + 4.0 + 5.0 + 7.0) ÷ 4 = 5.5
- Difference from baseline: 5.5 − 3.2 = +2.3

Scores after onion ran 2.3 points above your usual level, across 4 meals. This is a possible link only.

A second tag, "dairy", appears on only two meals with scores in the window. Two meals are too few to say anything, so a tag needs at least 3 meals before it is shown.

## How much to trust the result

- Sample size matters most. Three meals is the minimum. Ten or more is better.
- IBS symptoms change from day to day for many reasons. A gap of 0.5 points can be noise.
- Meals contain many foods. A tag that always appears with another food cannot be separated from it.
- The right window differs by person and by food. Try a different window and see whether the picture holds.
- Stress, sleep, hormones and medication are not part of this arithmetic.

A high result is a question to test. A dietitian can help you test it safely, for example with a structured low FODMAP reintroduction. Do not cut out whole food groups on the basis of a few meals.

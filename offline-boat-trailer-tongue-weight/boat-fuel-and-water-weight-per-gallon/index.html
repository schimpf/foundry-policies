Boat Fuel and Water Weight per Gallon Chart

Fuel and water are some of the heaviest things on a boat that you can change in minutes. Before you trailer the boat, you need their weight to check tongue weight, axle load and tow ratings. This page gives the weight per US gallon, a chart by gallons, and an example of what a full tank does to the load on the hitch.

Weight per US gallon

LiquidWeight per US gallon
Gasoline6.1 lb
Diesel7.1 lb
Fresh water8.34 lb
  • Water is about 8.34 lb per US gallon at room temperature. This is a standard physical value.
  • Gasoline and diesel vary a little with blend and temperature. Gasoline is commonly 6.0 to 6.3 lb per gallon and diesel about 7.0 to 7.2 lb per gallon. The values above are round figures for planning, and they are the ones the Boat Trailer Tongue Weight app uses.
  • These are US gallons. An imperial gallon is about 20 percent bigger. A liter of water is 1 kg, which is about 2.2 lb.

Partial tanks

Weight follows the fill level:

Weight = tank size in gallons x fill percent / 100 x weight per gallon

Example: a 40 gallon gasoline tank filled to 50 percent.

  • 40 x 50 / 100 = 20 gallons
  • 20 x 6.1 = 122 lb

A tank at 25 percent holds 10 gallons, so 61 lb of gasoline. A tank at 75 percent holds 30 gallons, so 183 lb.

Metric conversions

  • 1 US gallon = 3.785 liters
  • 1 lb = 0.4536 kg
  • Gasoline: 6.1 lb per gallon is about 0.73 kg per liter
  • Diesel: 7.1 lb per gallon is about 0.85 kg per liter
  • Water: 8.34 lb per gallon is about 1.00 kg per liter

Where the tank sits matters

On a trailer, weight does not only add to the total. Its position relative to the axle decides how much of it ends up on the hitch. A load behind the axle lifts the tongue. A load ahead of the axle pushes it down.

Worked example. A trailer has a coupler-to-axle distance of 168 inches. A 40 gallon gasoline tank, full, sits 40 inches behind the axle.

  1. Fuel weight = 40 x 6.1 = 244 lb.
  2. Moment = 244 x (-40) = -9,760 lb-in. The minus sign means behind the axle.
  3. Effect on tongue weight = -9,760 / 168 = -58 lb.

So a full tank behind the axle takes about 58 lb off the tongue. If the same tank is half full, the effect is about -29 lb. A tongue weight that passes with a full tank can fall short once you burn fuel, and the other way round.

Livewell and ballast water

A 30 gallon livewell holds 30 x 8.34 = 250.2 lb. If it sits 60 inches behind the axle, it lowers the tongue weight by 250.2 x 60 / 168 = about 89 lb. Draining it at the ramp can raise the tongue weight a lot. Water in a ballast tank ahead of the axle does the opposite.

Check the totals too

Fuel and water also add to:

  • Total trailer weight, against the trailer GVWR and the tow rating of the vehicle.
  • Axle load, against the trailer axle rating.
  • Combined weight, against the vehicle GCWR.

Do the check with the tanks as full as you will tow them, and again with them as empty as they will get.

Do it in the app

The Boat Trailer Tongue Weight app for Android works out liquid weight for you. Enter the tank size, pick gasoline, diesel or water, and move the level slider. The weight follows from the tank size and level, and the results update for the dry and the loaded trailer. It works offline.

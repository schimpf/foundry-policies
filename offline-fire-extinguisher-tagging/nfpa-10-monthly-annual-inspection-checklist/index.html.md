# NFPA 10 Monthly and Annual Inspection Checklist

> The 8 monthly and 16 annual fire extinguisher checks under NFPA 10, which items change by extinguisher type, and what to record when one fails.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-fire-extinguisher-tagging/nfpa-10-monthly-annual-inspection-checklist/
- App: Fire Extinguisher Inspection, https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-fire-extinguisher-tagging/index.md
## What NFPA 10 asks for

NFPA 10, Standard for Portable Fire Extinguishers (2022 edition), sets two routine checks for every portable extinguisher. Section 7.2 covers the monthly inspection, at intervals not longer than 31 days. Section 7.3 covers the annual maintenance check, once every 12 months. Your local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) can add rules, so check them as well.

The checklists below are the ones the app uses. The monthly list has 8 items. The annual list repeats those 8 and adds 8 more, so a full annual check has 16 items.

## Monthly checklist (8 items)

Each item is pass or fail.

1. Located in its designated place.
2. Visible, unobstructed and accessible.
3. Operating instructions legible and facing out.
4. Safety seal and tamper indicator intact.
5. Full by weight or hefting.
6. No damage, corrosion, leakage or clogged nozzle.
7. Pressure gauge in operable range.
8. Inspection tag updated.

### Gauge item and CO2

CO2 extinguishers have no pressure gauge, so item 7 does not apply to them. The app leaves it out for CO2 and shows 7 monthly items and 15 annual items. Every other type in the app (ABC, BC, clean agent, Class D dry powder, water, foam and Class K wet chemical) keeps the gauge item.

## Annual checklist (8 more items)

The annual check includes everything in the monthly list, plus:

1. Mechanical parts examined.
2. Extinguishing agent in good condition.
3. Expelling means checked.
4. Hose and nozzle in good condition.
5. Mounting bracket secure.
6. Nameplate and label legible.
7. 6-year and hydro dates within interval.
8. Maintenance tag attached.

Item 7 is the link to the longer service cycle. A unit can pass every other item and still fail here if its 6-year maintenance or hydrostatic test is past due. See the [interval chart](../fire-extinguisher-maintenance-and-hydrostatic-test-intervals/) for those dates.

## What to record when an item fails

A bare "fail" is hard to act on later. For each failed item, write down three things:

- **Reason:** what you saw, such as "gauge needle in the red" or "pin missing, seal broken".
- **Corrective action:** what was done or what is needed, such as "recharged on site" or "tagged out, sent to shop".
- **Replaced:** whether a different extinguisher now hangs in that spot.

Take a photo of the fault. Photos make a later report easier to defend, and 3 per inspection is usually enough: one of the whole unit, one of the fault, one of the tag.

## Worked example: a monthly round

You walk a small warehouse with 12 ABC extinguishers. At unit 7, the pressure gauge needle sits below the green band.

1. Mark items 1 to 6 pass.
2. Mark item 7 (gauge) fail. Reason: "needle below green". Corrective action: "removed for recharge, loaner installed". Replaced: yes.
3. Mark item 8 (tag updated) pass once you have initialed the tag.
4. Attach a photo of the gauge.

The inspection result for that unit is a fail. The other 11 pass. The report then lists one failure with its reason and photo, and 11 clean results.

## How the app picks monthly or annual

During an inspection round the app suggests the kind of check for each unit:

- Annual, when the annual date is due within 30 days or already overdue.
- Monthly, in every other case, including a unit with no annual date on record.

You can switch the kind before you answer the first item.

## Using the checklist in the app

Offline fire extinguisher tagging has both lists built in. Scan the QR code or barcode on the tag, and the app opens that extinguisher and its checklist. Failed items ask for a reason, and you can add a corrective action and the replaced flag. Everything stays on the phone, so it works in a basement or plant room with no signal. When the round is done, the app builds a PDF site report with every unit's result, which you sign on the screen.

The app records inspections. It does not replace a qualified technician or the rules of your AHJ.

How Much Paint Do I Need for a Room? Formula and Example

To find how much paint a room needs, work out the wall area, subtract doors and windows, multiply by the number of coats, divide by the coverage printed on the can, then add a margin for waste. This page shows each step with a worked example and a calculator you can use right here.

Paint calculator

The formula

  1. Gross wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height
  2. Openings = width × height × count, for every door and window
  3. Net wall area = gross wall area − openings
  4. Paint needed = net area × coats ÷ coverage × texture factor × (1 + waste %)

Coverage is the number on the can: how many square feet one gallon covers (or how many square meters one liter covers). Use the figure for the product you will actually buy. The app's starting value is 350 sq ft per gallon, or 10 m² per liter, which you can change.

Texture factor

Rough surfaces soak up more paint. The app uses these factors:

SurfaceFactor
Smooth1.00
Light texture1.10 (10% more)
Heavy texture1.20 (20% more)

Waste

Waste covers spills, roller loss, touch-ups and the paint left in the tray. The app starts at 10% and lets you set anything from 0% to 25%.

Worked example

A bedroom is 12 ft long, 14 ft wide and has 8 ft walls. It has one door (3 × 7 ft) and two windows (3 × 4 ft each). You want two coats of smooth finish, the can says 350 sq ft per gallon, and you allow 10% waste.

  1. Gross walls: 2 × (12 + 14) × 8 = 416 sq ft
  2. Openings: 21 + 12 + 12 = 45 sq ft
  3. Net walls: 416 − 45 = 371 sq ft
  4. Paint: 371 × 2 ÷ 350 = 2.12 gal, × 1.00 texture = 2.12 gal
  5. With waste: 2.12 × 1.10 = 2.33 gal

Round up to cans you can buy

Nobody sells 2.33 gallons. The app rounds up to the next quart, which gives 10 quarts, or 2 gallons and 2 quarts.

One rule matters here: if the rounding ends on 3 quarts, the app gives you one more gallon instead. A gallon usually costs less than three quarts, so 2 gallons and 3 quarts becomes 3 gallons.

In metric, the app rounds up to the next half liter and fills it with the largest tins first: 5 L, 2.5 L, 1 L and 0.5 L. For example, 9.22 L becomes 9.5 L, which is one 5 L tin, one 2.5 L tin and two 1 L tins.

The ceiling

The ceiling is length × width, so a 12 × 14 ft room has 168 sq ft. With two coats at the same coverage and 10% waste:

168 × 2 ÷ 350 × 1.10 = 1.06 gal, which rounds to 1 gallon and 1 quart.

Ceilings are usually painted separately from walls, so calculate them as their own line. The app does that: walls, ceiling, trim and primer each have their own switch, coat count and coverage.

Trim and primer

  • Trim: length of trim × a 4 inch band (0.1 m in metric). By default the length is the room perimeter. Texture does not add paint to trim.
  • Primer: net wall area, plus the ceiling when the ceiling is switched on.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the second coat. Most color changes need two. Coats multiply the whole result.
  • Using the wrong coverage. Coverage differs between products. Read the can, not a general chart.
  • Skipping the waste margin. Running out mid-wall can cause color differences between batches.
  • Rounding down. Always round up, then check whether the leftover is worth keeping for touch-ups.

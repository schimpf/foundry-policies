# Safe Internal Temperature Chart for Meat and Fish

> USDA minimum internal temperatures for beef, pork, lamb, chicken and fish, plus how chef-style doneness compares.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-steak-doneness-chart/safe-internal-temperature-chart-meat/
- App: Steak Doneness Chart & Timer, https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-steak-doneness-chart/index.md
This page lists the minimum internal temperatures the USDA gives for meat, poultry and fish, then compares them with the temperatures many cooks aim for. All numbers are the final temperature after resting.

## USDA safe minimum chart

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), "Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart".

| Food | Minimum | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Beef, pork, lamb: steaks, chops, roasts | 145°F (63°C) | 3 min |
| Fish | 145°F (63°C) | none listed |
| Ground beef, pork, lamb | 160°F (71°C) | none listed |
| Chicken and all poultry, including ground | 165°F (74°C) | none listed |

Measure with a food thermometer in the thickest part of the meat. Color is not a reliable sign of safety.

## Where common doneness levels fall

The table below shows the final temperatures from the Offline Steak Doneness Chart app next to the USDA minimum. A "below" means the final temperature is under the USDA number.

| Doneness | Final temp | Versus USDA |
|---|---|---|
| Rare | 125°F / 52°C | below |
| Medium rare | 135°F / 57°C | below |
| Medium | 145°F / 63°C | meets |
| Medium well | 150°F / 66°C | above |
| Well done | 160°F / 71°C | above |

### Burgers and ground meat (USDA 160°F)

| Doneness | Final temp | Versus USDA |
|---|---|---|
| Medium rare burger | 135°F / 57°C | below |
| Medium burger | 145°F / 63°C | below |
| Well done burger | 160°F / 71°C | meets |
| Ground pork or lamb | 160°F / 71°C | meets |
| Ground chicken | 165°F / 74°C | meets |

Ground meat is held to a higher number than whole cuts because grinding mixes surface bacteria through the whole patty.

### Pork (USDA 145°F)

| Cut and doneness | Final temp | Versus USDA |
|---|---|---|
| Chop or tenderloin, medium rare | 140°F / 60°C | below |
| Chop or tenderloin, medium | 145°F / 63°C | meets |
| Chop or tenderloin, well done | 155°F / 68°C | above |
| Ribs, tender | 198°F / 92°C | above |

Ribs are cooked far past the minimum so the meat turns tender.

### Chicken (USDA 165°F)

| Cut and doneness | Final temp | Versus USDA |
|---|---|---|
| Breast, juicy | 160°F / 71°C | below |
| Breast, USDA safe | 165°F / 74°C | meets |
| Thigh, tender | 180°F / 82°C | above |
| Thigh, fall apart | 195°F / 91°C | above |

Dark meat needs a higher temperature than breast to turn tender. Thighs are fine at 165°F for safety, but the connective tissue is still firm.

## Pull temperature versus final temperature

The USDA numbers are what the meat should read at the end. If you pull at the minimum, carryover pushes it higher. If you want the meat to land at 145°F, take it off about 5°F earlier for a steak and about 10°F earlier for a large roast. A chicken breast aimed at 165°F comes off at about 160°F.

## What the warnings mean

Restaurants serve medium rare steak every day, so a temperature below the USDA minimum does not make every steak unsafe for every person. The USDA minimum is the level the agency recommends, and the risk is higher for pregnant people, young children, older adults and anyone with a weakened immune system. For ground meat and poultry the risk is higher still, since bacteria can be inside the product as well as on the surface.

Practical rules:

- Cook poultry and ground meat to the USDA number.
- Sear whole steaks on every side if you choose to go below the minimum.
- Use a fresh, properly stored cut and a clean thermometer.
- When in doubt, cook it to the USDA number.

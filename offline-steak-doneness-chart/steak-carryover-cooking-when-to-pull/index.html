Carryover Cooking: When to Pull Steak Off the Heat

You take a steak off the grill at 135°F, and by the time you slice it, it reads 140°F or more. That rise is carryover cooking. If you plan for it, you pull earlier and the steak lands exactly where you want it.

The formula

Pull temperature = target final temperature − carryover rise

The carryover rise depends on the size of the cut:

CutTypical riseExample
Steak, chop, thin cutabout 5°F (3°C)pull at 130°F for 135°F
Large roastabout 10°F (5–6°C)pull at 125°F for 135°F
Brisketabout 3°F (2°C)pull at 200°F for 203°F
Ground meat pattyabout 5°F (3°C)pull at 155°F for 160°F

These are the gaps used in the Offline Steak Doneness Chart app. They are rules of thumb. A thick steak in a hot cast-iron pan can rise more, and a thin steak on a cool grill can rise less.

Worked example: a medium rare ribeye

  1. You want a final temperature of 135°F (57.2°C).
  2. A ribeye steak rises about 5°F (2.8°C).
  3. Pull temperature = 135 − 5 = 130°F. In Celsius: 57.2 − 2.8 = 54.4°C.
  4. Take it off at 130°F. Rest it 5 minutes.
  5. After the rest it should read about 135°F.

Worked example: a medium rare prime rib

  1. Target final temperature: 135°F (57°C).
  2. A big roast rises about 10°F (5.6°C).
  3. Pull temperature = 135 − 10 = 125°F (52°C).
  4. Rest it 20 minutes, loosely covered.

If you pulled the roast at 135°F, it would end near 145°F, which is medium instead of medium rare.

Converting °F and °C

You may be working from a recipe in the other unit. The formulas are:

  • °F = °C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32
  • °C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9

Check with the ribeye: 54.4 × 9 ÷ 5 + 32 = 97.92 + 32 = 129.92, which rounds to 130°F.

Go the other way: (130 − 32) × 5 ÷ 9 = 98 × 5 ÷ 9 = 54.4°C.

The app stores every temperature in tenths of a degree Celsius and converts for display, then rounds to a whole degree. A pull temp of 54.4°C therefore shows as 54°C or 130°F. Differences of a fraction of a degree are below what a kitchen thermometer can resolve.

Why resting matters

Resting does two jobs. The temperature evens out, so the center catches up with the outside. The juices also settle, so they stay in the meat when you cut it. Rest times in the app run from 2 minutes for tuna to 60 minutes for brisket. Steaks and chops get 5 minutes, roasts 15 to 20, ground meat 3.

Check against food safety

Carryover helps you hit a final temperature, but it does not change the safe minimum. USDA FSIS lists 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest for whole cuts of beef, pork and lamb, and 160°F (71°C) for ground meat (source: USDA FSIS, "Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart"). A medium rare steak finishing at 135°F is below that figure. You decide the risk. The app flags it so you can see it.

Tips for accurate pulls

  • Keep the probe in the center of the thickest part.
  • Watch the number climb during the last few minutes and note how fast it moves.
  • Do not leave a roast uncovered in a cold kitchen. It will rise less.
  • Do not wrap a steak tightly. Trapped steam can push the rise higher and soften the crust.

# Steak Doneness Temperature Chart (°F and °C)

> Pull and final temperatures for rare to well done steak in °F and °C, with rest times and the USDA safe minimum explained.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-steak-doneness-chart/steak-doneness-temperature-chart/
- App: Steak Doneness Chart & Timer, https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-steak-doneness-chart/index.md
Use this chart to know when to take a steak off the heat. Every row gives two numbers: the pull temperature (when you remove the steak) and the final temperature (where it lands after resting). The two differ because the steak keeps cooking for a few minutes after it leaves the pan or grill.

## Steak doneness chart

These values apply to ribeye, strip, filet mignon and sirloin. Rest each steak 5 minutes.

| Doneness | Pull temp | Final temp | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rare | 120°F / 49°C | 125°F / 52°C | 5 min |
| Medium rare | 130°F / 54°C | 135°F / 57°C | 5 min |
| Medium | 140°F / 60°C | 145°F / 63°C | 5 min |
| Medium well | 145°F / 63°C | 150°F / 66°C | 5 min |
| Well done | 155°F / 68°C | 160°F / 71°C | 5 min |

Flank and skirt steak use the same numbers for rare, medium rare and medium. They are thin, lean cuts that turn tough when cooked past medium.

## Roasts and thick cuts

Large cuts hold more heat, so they climb further while resting. Pull them earlier and rest them longer.

| Cut and doneness | Pull temp | Final temp | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prime rib roast, rare | 115°F / 46°C | 125°F / 52°C | 20 min |
| Prime rib roast, medium rare | 125°F / 52°C | 135°F / 57°C | 20 min |
| Prime rib roast, medium | 135°F / 57°C | 145°F / 63°C | 20 min |
| Brisket, tender and sliced | 200°F / 93°C | 203°F / 95°C | 60 min |

Brisket is cooked far past "done" so the connective tissue breaks down. It has no rare or medium.

## How to measure

1. Use a thermometer you have checked in ice water (it should read 32°F / 0°C).
2. Insert the tip into the thickest part, away from bone and fat.
3. For a thin steak, slide the probe in from the side so the tip sits in the center.
4. Check early and often. Fifteen degrees go by fast on a hot grill.
5. Remove the steak at the pull temperature, not the final temperature.

## What the USDA says

The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) lists 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest as the safe minimum internal temperature for whole cuts of beef, pork and lamb (source: USDA FSIS, "Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart"). Ground beef needs 160°F (71°C).

Compare that with the chart:

- Rare and medium rare finish below 145°F. They are common restaurant and chef choices, and they are under the USDA minimum.
- Medium finishes at 145°F, which meets the minimum.
- Medium well and well done finish above it.

Higher risk groups, such as pregnant people, young children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems, should follow the USDA number. The chart is guidance only. Your own judgment and a reliable thermometer matter more than any table.

## Why the pull temperature is lower

Heat is still moving from the hot outside of the steak toward the cooler center when you take it off. A standard steak gains roughly 5°F (3°C) while it rests. A large roast can gain 10°F (5–6°C). If you pull at your target, you overshoot it. The page on carryover cooking has a worked example.

## Which cut to pick

- Ribeye: well marbled, forgiving, good at medium rare to medium.
- Strip: firmer bite, a good all-rounder.
- Filet mignon: very lean and tender. It dries out quickly past medium.
- Sirloin: lean and budget friendly. Rest it and slice across the grain.

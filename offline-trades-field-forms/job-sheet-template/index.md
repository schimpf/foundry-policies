# Job Sheet Template: What to Include and Why

> The fields a tradesperson's job sheet needs, what to write in each one, and how to get it signed and sent to the client the same day.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-trades-field-forms/job-sheet-template/
- App: Field Forms: Job Sheets & PDFs, https://apps.schimpf.es/offline-trades-field-forms/index.md
A job sheet is the short record of one visit: who asked for what, what you did, what you used and how long it took. It settles arguments later ("I never asked for that"), makes invoicing quick and gives you proof of the work. This page lists the fields that matter and what to write in each.

A job sheet is a general-purpose record. It is not a certificate. Gas safety records, electrical installation condition reports and similar forms are regulated documents with their own required layouts. Use the official forms for those.

## The fields to include

### Header: who, where, when

- **Date** of the visit.
- **Client name and site address.** The address of the work, which may differ from the client's billing address.
- **Arrival time** and **departure time.** These back up your labor hours if a client questions the bill.

### The work

- **Work requested.** Write what the client asked for, in their words. If the scope grows on site, this field shows what was originally agreed.
- **Work carried out.** Say what you did, in plain steps. "Replaced failed pump, bled system, checked pressure" is better than "repairs".
- **Materials used.** List parts with quantities. This feeds the invoice lines directly.
- **Labor hours.** A decimal number: 1.5 means one and a half hours. Match it to arrival and departure times.

### Follow-up

- **Further work needed.** A yes/no answer. If yes, add **details**: what is needed, why, and any quote you promised.
- **Notes.** Access problems, client requests, pets, parking, anything the next visit needs to know.

### Sign-off

- **Client signature.** Ask the client to sign when you finish, while you are still on site. It confirms they were present and saw the work.
- **Your signature.** Add your own, so the document is complete.
- **Photos.** Take before and after pictures and add a short caption to each, such as "Under-sink leak before repair".

## A short worked example

A plumber attends a leaking tap on 12 June.

1. Date: 12 June. Arrival 09:10, departure 10:40.
2. Work requested: "Kitchen tap drips constantly."
3. Work carried out: "Isolated supply, replaced cartridge, tested for leaks, restored supply."
4. Materials: "1 x tap cartridge."
5. Labor hours: 1.5 (09:10 to 10:40).
6. Further work needed: no.
7. Notes: "Client has a second tap with a worn handle, may want a quote."
8. Two photos with captions. Client signs.

## Habits that make job sheets useful

- **Fill it in on site**, not that evening. Details fade quickly.
- **One sheet per visit.** A job with three visits has three sheets.
- **Give exact times and quantities.** Vague entries invite questions.
- **Do not edit after signing.** If something changes, make a copy and note the change.
- **Keep photos relevant.** A few clear ones with captions beat thirty unlabeled ones.
- **Send the same day.** A prompt PDF means a prompt invoice.

## Paper versus phone

Paper job sheets get wet, lost and left in the van. A phone is already in your pocket, so the sheet, photos and signature can live in one place, tied to the right job.

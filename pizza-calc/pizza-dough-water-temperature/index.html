Pizza Dough Water Temperature Calculator and Formula

Dough that ends mixing too warm ferments too fast. Dough that ends too cold ferments slowly and can feel stiff. The fix is simple: pick a target dough temperature, then choose the water temperature that gets you there. This page gives the formula, the numbers for each mixing method and a worked example.

The short answer

Pick a finished dough temperature, for example 24 °C (75 °F), then work out the water temperature with the formula below. The right water is often cooler than tap water in summer and warmer in winter.

The formula

The method is the "desired dough temperature" method from classic bread baking (see Jeffrey Hamelman, *Bread: A Baker's Book of Techniques and Recipes*, 2nd edition, 2012). The dough's final temperature is roughly the average of everything that goes into it, plus the heat from mixing.

Straight dough (flour, water, yeast):

Water = 3 × target dough − (flour temperature + room temperature + friction)

Dough with a preferment (poolish, biga or starter):

Water = 4 × target dough − (flour temperature + room temperature + friction + preferment temperature)

The multiplier is the number of temperatures you average: three for a straight dough, four when a preferment is added. All temperatures are in °C. For °F, convert your numbers to °C first, or use the calculator below.

Friction values

Mixing adds heat. The values this page and the app use are:

Mixing methodFriction added
By hand1 °C
Spiral mixer11 °C
Planetary (stand) mixer13 °C

These are typical planning values, not measurements of your machine. A long mix or a hot bowl adds more. If your dough keeps coming out warm or cold, adjust the friction number by a degree or two and note it.

Worked example

You make a straight dough in a planetary mixer. The kitchen is 21 °C and the flour sits at room temperature, 21 °C. You want 24 °C dough.

  1. Multiply the target by 3: 3 × 24 = 72.
  2. Add flour, room and friction: 21 + 21 + 13 = 55.
  3. Subtract: 72 − 55 = 17 °C water.

Same kitchen, same target, different mixing:

Mixing methodCalculationWater temperature
By hand72 − (21 + 21 + 1)29 °C
Spiral mixer72 − (21 + 21 + 11)19 °C
Planetary mixer72 − (21 + 21 + 13)17 °C

Now add a biga held at 21 °C, planetary mixer:

  1. 4 × 24 = 96.
  2. 21 + 21 + 13 + 21 = 76.
  3. 96 − 76 = 20 °C water.

Tips for using the result

  • Measure the flour. Flour kept in a cold cellar or a warm cupboard is not at room temperature. Use a probe thermometer.
  • Check the dough right after mixing. Each 1 °C of error in the dough is worth 3 °C of water in a straight dough, or 4 °C with a preferment. If the dough lands 2 °C too warm, use water 6 °C cooler (8 °C with a preferment) next time.
  • Very low or very high results. If the formula asks for water below about 1 °C or above 60 °C, change the plan. Use ice for part of the water, or lower the target. Water above about 60 °C can harm yeast.
  • Cold fermentation. The target applies to the dough coming out of the mixer. The fridge stage comes after.
  • Yeast amount. A warmer dough ferments faster, so the same recipe needs less yeast. Plan temperature and yeast together.

Limits

  • The formula treats flour, water and preferment as equal in heat capacity. That is close, not exact.
  • Friction depends on mixer, speed, mixing time and dough stiffness.
  • Room temperature stands in for the flour and the preferment when you have no better reading.

Use it in Pizza Calc

Pizza Calc works out the water temperature for you. Enter your target dough temperature, room temperature and mixing method (hand, spiral or planetary) in Settings. The Calculator tab then shows the water temperature, and switches to the four-temperature formula when you use a preferment. The app keeps the result between 1 °C and 60 °C.

# Retirement Weekly Schedule Template With Example

> A sample weekly schedule for the first months of retirement, with hours per category and how to adapt it to your own week.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/retirement-purpose-planner/retirement-weekly-schedule-template/
- App: Purpose Planner: Retirement, https://apps.schimpf.es/retirement-purpose-planner/index.md
After a few weeks, retirement can feel loose. Work used to set the shape of the week, and now nothing does. A simple weekly schedule brings back some rhythm without turning your days into a timetable.

This page gives a full example week for the first months of retirement, shows how to add up the hours per category, and explains how to make it your own.

## What a retirement schedule needs

A useful schedule is short and repeats. Aim for blocks you can keep up. Six categories cover most weeks:

- Routine: the anchors of the day, such as coffee, planning the day, chores.
- Exercise: walking, stretching, swimming, a class.
- Social: time with friends and family.
- Project: a hobby, something you are learning, a home project.
- Volunteering: regular time for a cause or an organization.
- Rest: a nap, reading, quiet time. Rest is part of the plan.

Each block has a title, a category, the days it happens and a start and end time.

## Example week: the first 90 days

This is the "First 90 days of retirement" template from the Retirement Purpose Planner app. It is a gentle rhythm with daily movement, time with people, one project and room to rest.

| Block | Category | Days | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning walk | Exercise | Mon, Wed, Fri | 07:30–08:15 |
| Coffee and the paper | Routine | Every day | 09:00–09:30 |
| Plan the day | Routine | Mon–Fri | 09:30–09:45 |
| Project time | Project | Tue, Thu | 10:00–11:30 |
| Try a volunteering taster | Volunteering | Wed | 10:00–12:00 |
| Lunch with a friend | Social | Fri | 12:30–14:00 |
| Afternoon rest | Rest | Mon–Fri | 15:00–15:30 |
| Stretching | Exercise | Tue, Thu, Sat | 17:00–17:30 |
| Call family | Social | Sun | 16:00–17:00 |

The example also suggests two starter projects: "Write my purpose statement" and "Find a volunteering role", each with four small milestones.

## How to add up the hours

For each block, multiply its length in minutes by the number of days it happens in a week. Then add the blocks in the same category.

1. Morning walk: 45 minutes × 3 days = 135 minutes.
2. Stretching: 30 minutes × 3 days = 90 minutes.
3. Exercise total: 135 + 90 = 225 minutes.

The same sum for every category in the example week:

| Category | Minutes per week | Hours (nearest quarter) |
|---|---|---|
| Routine | 285 | 4.75 |
| Exercise | 225 | 3.75 |
| Project | 180 | 3 |
| Social | 150 | 2.5 |
| Rest | 150 | 2.5 |
| Volunteering | 120 | 2 |
| **Total** | **1,110** | **18.5** |

So the example fills about 18.5 hours of a week. That leaves most of the week open on purpose. It is easy to over-plan the first weeks and then feel you failed. Start light and add blocks later.

## Check the exercise block against a guideline

The World Health Organization's *Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour* (2020) recommend that adults aged 65 and over do at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity a week, or the vigorous equivalent. They also suggest muscle-strengthening activities on two or more days and balance-focused activity on three or more days for older adults.

The example's 225 minutes is planned time and says nothing about intensity. Whether a walk counts as moderate depends on your pace and health. Ask your doctor what is right for you.

## Make it your own

1. Start from what you already do. Write down your current week. Put those blocks in first.
2. Keep a few fixed anchors. A set wake-up time, a daily walk and one weekly social plan give the week a spine.
3. Add one project. One is enough. Break it into milestones small enough to finish in a session.
4. Try volunteering before committing. The example uses a two-hour "taster" for this reason.
5. Protect rest. The example has a 30-minute rest block on weekdays.
6. Review weekly. Once a week, ask which blocks you kept, which you skipped and why. Change the blocks that did not work.

## A weekly check-in

A plan only helps if you look back at it. At the end of each week, compare planned time to what you actually did, by category. Then give the week a score from 1 to 5 for how purposeful it felt, and add a line about why. After a few weeks you will see which blocks give you energy.

Retirement Purpose Planner turns this example into your own plan on Android. Apply the "First 90 days of retirement" starter template, then edit any block. It then:

It works offline, has no account, and stores everything on your device. It runs on Android 8.0 and later and is in English. Find it on Google Play as "Purpose Planner: Retirement".

How to Scale a Sourdough Recipe With Baker's Percentages

Scale a sourdough recipe to a target dough or flour weight. One factor, a worked example, and why hydration stays the same.

You have a recipe for one loaf and you want two, or you have a pan that needs exactly 900 g of dough. With baker's percentages you find one factor and multiply every ingredient by it.

Baker's percentages in one minute

Every ingredient is written as a percentage of the recipe flour. The recipe flour is 100 %. If the flour is 500 g and the water is 350 g, the water is 70 %. Because every ingredient is tied to flour, the percentages do not change when you scale. Only the weights change.

Scaling by one factor

  1. Pick what you are scaling to: a target dough weight or a target flour weight.
  2. Work out the current weight of that thing.
  3. Factor = target / current.
  4. Multiply every ingredient by the factor.
  5. Round to whole grams.

Hydration, salt percentage and starter percentage stay as they were, because every weight went through the same multiplication.

Which weight counts as flour?

For a flour target, the base is total flour: the recipe flour plus the flour inside the starter. Using only the recipe flour gives a different factor when your starter is large.

The flour in the starter is: starter weight / (1 + starter hydration / 100).

Worked example: scale to a target dough weight

The base recipe:

IngredientWeight
Bread flour450 g
Whole wheat flour50 g
Water350 g
Starter at 100 % hydration100 g
Salt10 g

Total dough = 960 g. You want 1200 g.

  1. Factor = 1200 / 960 = 1.25.
  2. Multiply each weight by 1.25.
IngredientOldx 1.25Rounded
Bread flour450 g562.5 g563 g
Whole wheat flour50 g62.5 g63 g
Water350 g437.5 g438 g
Starter100 g125 g125 g
Salt10 g12.5 g13 g

The rounded weights add up to 1202 g instead of 1200 g. This is normal. Each ingredient is rounded to 1 g on its own, so the total can drift by a few grams. If you need an exact total, adjust the largest ingredient by the difference.

Worked example: scale to a target flour weight

Same recipe. Total flour is 500 g of recipe flour plus 50 g from the starter, so 550 g. You want 660 g of total flour.

  1. Factor = 660 / 550 = 1.2.
  2. Bread flour 450 x 1.2 = 540 g.
  3. Whole wheat flour 50 x 1.2 = 60 g.
  4. Water 350 x 1.2 = 420 g.
  5. Starter 100 x 1.2 = 120 g.
  6. Salt 10 x 1.2 = 12 g.

New dough weight is 540 + 60 + 420 + 120 + 12 = 1152 g. Check the hydration: recipe water is 420 g over recipe flour of 600 g, which is 70 %. Same as before.

Ounces

If your scale uses ounces, convert first. One ounce is 28.3495 g. To convert grams to ounces, divide by 28.3495. To convert ounces to grams, multiply. Scaling works the same in either unit, but grams are easier to round.

Example: 1200 g is 1200 / 28.3495 = 42.3 oz.

Check list before you bake a scaled recipe

  • Does the dough weight fit your pan or banneton?
  • Does the larger dough need a longer bulk? Bigger dough holds heat differently, so watch the dough as well as the clock.
  • Is the starter still the same percentage of flour? It should be, if you scaled it with the rest.

Let the app do the multiplication

Sourdough Hydration Journal is an Android app with a calculator that scales a recipe to a target dough weight or a target flour weight.

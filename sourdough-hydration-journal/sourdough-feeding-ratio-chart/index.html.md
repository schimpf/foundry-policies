# Sourdough Starter Feeding Ratio Chart (1:1:1, 1:2:2, 1:5:5)

> Chart of starter, flour and water weights for 1:1:1, 1:2:2 and 1:5:5 feedings, with a worked example and the hydration formula.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/sourdough-hydration-journal/sourdough-feeding-ratio-chart/
- App: Sourdough Hydration Journal, https://apps.schimpf.es/sourdough-hydration-journal/index.md
A feeding ratio tells you how much flour and water to add for each part of starter. It is written starter : flour : water. This page has a chart of the three common ratios, the formula behind it and a worked example for a stiffer or wetter feed.

## How to read a ratio

In 1:2:2, one part is the starter you keep, two parts are flour and two parts are water, all by weight. If you keep 20 g of starter, you add 40 g of flour and 40 g of water.

- Flour to add = starter weight x flour parts / starter parts
- Water to add = starter weight x water parts / starter parts
- Total after feeding = starter + flour + water

Always weigh in grams. Cups and spoons are not reliable for flour.

## Feeding ratio chart

Flour and water amounts for common weights of kept starter. Flour and water are equal in each ratio.

| Kept starter | 1:1:1 flour + water | 1:1:1 total | 1:2:2 flour + water | 1:2:2 total | 1:5:5 flour + water | 1:5:5 total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 g | 10 g + 10 g | 30 g | 20 g + 20 g | 50 g | 50 g + 50 g | 110 g |
| 20 g | 20 g + 20 g | 60 g | 40 g + 40 g | 100 g | 100 g + 100 g | 220 g |
| 50 g | 50 g + 50 g | 150 g | 100 g + 100 g | 250 g | 250 g + 250 g | 550 g |
| 100 g | 100 g + 100 g | 300 g | 200 g + 200 g | 500 g | 500 g + 500 g | 1100 g |

The ratio is a multiplier. The total is always the kept starter times (1 + flour parts + water parts): 3 for 1:1:1, 5 for 1:2:2 and 11 for 1:5:5.

## Work backwards from the amount you need

If a recipe needs 200 g of ripe starter and you feed at 1:2:2:

1. Total parts = 1 + 2 + 2 = 5.
2. Kept starter = 200 / 5 = 40 g.
3. Flour = 80 g. Water = 80 g.

Add 40 g of starter to 80 g flour and 80 g water and you get 200 g. If you also want a little extra for the next feeding, raise the total before you divide.

## What the ratio does to hydration

Hydration here means water over flour, with the flour and water already in the kept starter included. For a seed starter at 100 % hydration and a feeding with equal flour and water, the result stays at 100 %. If the water is lower than the flour, the starter becomes stiffer.

The formula, with the seed starter at hydration H (in percent):

1. Seed flour = seed weight / (1 + H / 100).
2. Seed water = seed weight - seed flour.
3. Hydration after feeding = (seed water + added water) / (seed flour + added flour) x 100.

### Worked example: a stiffer feed

Keep 20 g of a 100 % starter and feed it at 1:5:4. That means 100 g of flour and 80 g of water.

- Seed flour = 20 / 2 = 10 g. Seed water = 10 g.
- Total flour = 10 + 100 = 110 g.
- Total water = 10 + 80 = 90 g.
- Hydration = 90 / 110 = 81.8 %.

The total weight is 20 + 100 + 80 = 200 g.

### Worked example: a wetter feed

Keep 20 g of a 100 % starter and feed it at 1:2:3. That means 40 g of flour and 60 g of water.

- Total flour = 10 + 40 = 50 g.
- Total water = 10 + 60 = 70 g.
- Hydration = 70 / 50 = 140.0 %.

## Which ratio should I pick?

The ratio sets how much fresh flour each bit of starter gets, so it changes how much food is available.

- 1:1:1 is a small dilution. The starter has less fresh flour per part, which tends to make it hungry again sooner.
- 1:2:2 is a middle option and a common default.
- 1:5:5 dilutes the starter a lot. It has more fresh flour per part, so it generally takes longer to peak.

Exact timing depends on your flour, your kitchen temperature and your starter. Log your own peak times to see what a ratio does in your kitchen.

## Keep track of your feedings

Sourdough Hydration Journal is an Android app with a feeding log built around these ratios. One tap logs a feeding. The presets are 1:1:1, 1:2:2 and 1:5:5, and you can set your own ratio. You can add a note and the time your starter peaked. The app remembers your usual ratio and starter weight, and can send a reminder at the interval you choose for each starter. It works offline and needs no account.

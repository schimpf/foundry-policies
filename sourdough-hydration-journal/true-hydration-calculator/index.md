# Sourdough True Hydration Calculator With Starter

> Work out sourdough hydration including the flour and water inside your starter. Formula, worked example and a small calculator.

- Web: https://apps.schimpf.es/sourdough-hydration-journal/true-hydration-calculator/
- App: Sourdough Hydration Journal, https://apps.schimpf.es/sourdough-hydration-journal/index.md
Most sourdough recipes give one hydration number. That number usually ignores the flour and water in the starter. If you want to know how wet your dough really is, you need the true hydration. This page shows the formula, a worked example and a calculator you can use right here.

## Formula hydration vs. true hydration

- Formula hydration is the recipe water divided by the recipe flour. The starter is left out.
- True hydration is all the water divided by all the flour. The flour and water inside the starter are included.

Both are expressed as a percentage of flour weight. That is the idea behind baker's percentages, where flour is always 100 %.

The two numbers differ because a starter is flour and water that have already been mixed and fed. A 100 g starter at 100 % hydration holds 50 g of flour and 50 g of water. Those 100 g go into the dough as well.

## The steps

1. Find the flour in the starter: starter flour = starter weight / (1 + starter hydration / 100).
2. Find the water in the starter: starter water = starter weight - starter flour.
3. Add up the flour: total flour = recipe flour + starter flour.
4. Add up the water: total water = recipe water + starter water, plus any liquid that counts as water, such as milk.
5. Divide: true hydration = total water / total flour x 100.

## Worked example

A loaf with these ingredients:

| Ingredient | Weight |
|---|---|
| Bread flour | 450 g |
| Whole wheat flour | 50 g |
| Water | 350 g |
| Starter at 100 % hydration | 100 g |
| Salt | 10 g |

Formula hydration. Recipe flour is 450 + 50 = 500 g. Recipe water is 350 g. So 350 / 500 = 70.0 %.

True hydration.

1. Starter flour = 100 / (1 + 100 / 100) = 50 g.
2. Starter water = 100 - 50 = 50 g.
3. Total flour = 500 + 50 = 550 g.
4. Total water = 350 + 50 = 400 g.
5. True hydration = 400 / 550 = 72.7 %.

Total dough weight is 500 + 350 + 100 + 10 = 960 g. The salt is part of neither the flour nor the water, so it only adds to dough weight.

### Baker's percentages for the same loaf

Baker's percentages use the recipe flour (500 g) as the base:

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 450 g | 90.0 % |
| Whole wheat flour | 50 g | 10.0 % |
| Water | 350 g | 70.0 % |
| Starter | 100 g | 20.0 % |
| Salt | 10 g | 2.0 % |

## The starter hydration changes the result

Keep the same recipe, but use a stiff starter at 50 % hydration:

- Starter flour = 100 / 1.5 = 66.7 g.
- Starter water = 33.3 g.
- Total flour = 566.7 g. Total water = 383.3 g.
- True hydration = 383.3 / 566.7 = 67.6 %.

The recipe list looks identical, and formula hydration is still 70.0 %. The dough is about five points drier than the 100 % starter version. If you swap starters between bakes, true hydration is the number that tells you what changed.

## Liquids other than water

Milk, buttermilk and similar liquids are mostly water. If you want them in the total, add them to the water side. In the calculator below, use the milk field. The weight is added to total water and to total dough weight. Eggs, oil and honey are different, so leave them out of that field unless you want to treat them as water yourself.

## Common questions

### Which number should I use when I follow a recipe?

Use the one the recipe uses. Most printed recipes quote formula hydration. If a recipe says 75 % and ignores the starter, your dough will be a bit wetter than 75 % by true hydration.

### What if I have no starter in the dough?

Then both numbers match. Set the starter weight to 0.

### Does salt count?

No. Salt is a percentage of flour like the other ingredients, but it is neither flour nor water.

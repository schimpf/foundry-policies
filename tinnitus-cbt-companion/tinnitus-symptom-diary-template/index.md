## What a tinnitus diary is for

A tinnitus diary is a short daily record of how your tinnitus affects you. After a few weeks it shows whether a sound routine, a lesson or a change in sleep is making a difference. It also gives your doctor numbers to read.

## What to rate each day

Use four scores from 0 to 10. Rate once a day, at the same time, so the numbers are comparable.

| Score | 0 means | 10 means |
|---|---|---|
| Loudness | Silent | Very loud |
| Annoyance | Not at all | Unbearable |
| Sleep quality (last night) | Very poor | Very good |
| Stress | Calm | Extreme |

Loudness is how loud the tinnitus seems. Annoyance is how much it bothers you. They can differ: a quiet tinnitus can still be very annoying on a bad day. Sleep is the only score where a high number is good.

Add a short note when something stands out, such as a loud concert, a poor night or a new medicine.

## How to read the trend

Looking at one day tells you little. Two numbers help over a week or a month:

1. **The average.** Add the scores and divide by the number of days you logged.
2. **The direction.** Split your logged scores into an earlier half and a later half and compare the averages.

This page uses the same rule as the Tinnitus CBT Companion app:

- You need at least 4 logged days.
- Take the number of scores, divide by 2, and round down. That many scores form the first half. All remaining scores form the second half (so with an odd count, the extra score goes in the second half).
- Subtract the first-half average from the second-half average.
- A difference above +0.5 is trending up. Below −0.5 is trending down. Anything in between is steady.

Days you skipped are left out, not counted as zero.

### Worked example

Annoyance for 7 days: 7, 6, 7, 5, 5, 4, 4.

- Average: (7 + 6 + 7 + 5 + 5 + 4 + 4) ÷ 7 = 38 ÷ 7 = 5.4
- Count 7, divided by 2 and rounded down is 3. First half: 7, 6, 7, average 6.7.
- Second half: 5, 5, 4, 4, average 4.5.
- Difference: 4.5 − 6.7 = −2.2, which is below −0.5, so the trend is down.

For loudness, annoyance and stress, down is the direction you want. For sleep quality, up is better.

## Take the diary to your doctor

Bring the average and direction for each score, plus your notes. If your tinnitus is new, changed suddenly, is in one ear only, beats in time with your pulse, or comes with hearing loss, dizziness or pain, see a doctor first.

A diary is also a standard part of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for tinnitus. The guideline *Tinnitus* from the American Academy of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery Foundation (2014, Tunkel et al.) recommends CBT for persistent, bothersome tinnitus.
